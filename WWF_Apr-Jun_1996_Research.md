# WWF April-June 1996 Research Report

**Period Covered:** April 1, 1996 - June 30, 1996

## Executive Summary

The April-June 1996 period marked a pivotal transitional phase in WWF, bridging the end of the "New Generation" era and the dawn of the "Attitude Era." The period featured three major PPV events: In Your House 7: Good Friends, Better Enemies (April 28, Omaha), In Your House 8: Beware of Dog (May 26/28, Florence/North Charleston - two-part event due to power outage), and King of the Ring 1996 (June 23, Milwaukee). The most significant development was "Stone Cold" Steve Austin winning the King of the Ring tournament and delivering his iconic "Austin 3:16" promo, which is widely cited by WWE as the starting point of the Attitude Era. Shawn Michaels continued his first WWF Championship reign, successfully defending against Diesel at In Your House 7 and British Bulldog at King of the Ring. Ahmed Johnson won the Intercontinental Championship from Goldust at King of the Ring. The period also saw the WWF debut of Brian Pillman, the beginning of the Mankind vs. Undertaker feud, and Ted DiBiase's departure from WWF after losing a Caribbean Strap match to Savio Vega at In Your House 8. This period marked the beginning of Steve Austin's rise to superstardom and the shift toward the edgier, more controversial content that would define the Attitude Era.

## Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### The Curtain Call Incident (May 19, 1996)

**Incident:** The Kliq (Shawn Michaels, Kevin Nash/Diesel, Scott Hall/Razor Ramon, Hunter Hearst Helmsley/Triple H) broke kayfabe at a house show

**Details:**
- Date: May 19, 1996, at a house show in Madison Square Garden
- The Kliq broke kayfabe by hugging and interacting as themselves, not their characters
- This was a rare breach of kayfabe and was seen as unprofessional
- As punishment, Hunter Hearst Helmsley was stripped of his planned King of the Ring victory
- Steve Austin was given the King of the Ring victory instead
- **Source:** https://medium.com/@john.e.capcom/what-if-triple-h-had-won-the-1996-king-of-the-ring-alternative-wrestling-history-b2be2a9ac9ac

### Steve Austin's Lip Injury

**Incident:** Austin's lip was lacerated during King of the Ring semi-final match

**Details:**
- Date: June 23, 1996, at King of the Ring
- Marc Mero whipped Austin through the ropes and backdropped him
- Austin's lip was badly lacerated and required stitches
- Austin went to the hospital and received nearly 16 stitches in his mouth and tongue between matches
- He returned for the final match against Jake Roberts with his stitches still bleeding
- **Source:** https://www.wrestleview.com/retro/385977-wwf-king-of-the-ring-results-june-23-1996-milwaukee/

- A severe thunderstorm caused the power to go out during the event

### "Austin 3:16" Promo

**Incident:** Steve Austin delivered iconic promo after winning King of the Ring

**Details:**
- Date: June 23, 1996, at King of the Ring
- Austin mocked Jake Roberts' Bible-preacher gimmick
- Austin said: "You sit there and you thump your Bible, and you say your prayers, and it didn't get you anywhere! Talk about your psalms, talk about John 3:16... Austin 3:16 says I just whipped your ass!"
- This promo is widely cited by WWE as the starting point of the Attitude Era
- The "Austin 3:16" T-shirt sold over 400,000 units in its first year
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Ring_(1996)

### Shawn Michaels' Post-WrestleMania Behavior

**Incident:** Michaels' behavior after winning WWF Championship at WrestleMania XII

**Details:**
- After defeating Bret Hart in the Iron Man match at WrestleMania XII, Michaels told Hart to "get the hell out of my ring"
- This wasn't part of the agreed-upon finish
- Hart was frustrated and took a six-month hiatus
- This marked the beginning of their real-life animosity, which would culminate in the Montreal Screwjob at Survivor Series 1997

## Conclusion

The April-June 1996 period was a pivotal transitional phase in WWF, marking the end of the "New Generation" era and the dawn of the "Attitude Era." The period featured three major PPV events, with the most significant being King of the Ring 1996, where "Stone Cold" Steve Austin won the tournament and delivered his iconic "Austin 3:16" promo. Shawn Michaels continued his first WWF Championship reign, successfully defending against Diesel and British Bulldog. Ahmed Johnson won the Intercontinental Championship from Goldust at King of the Ring. The period also saw the WWF debut of Brian Pillman, the beginning of the Mankind vs. Undertaker feud, and Ted DiBiase's departure from WWW. This period marked the beginning of Steve Austin's rise to superstardom and the shift toward the edgier, more controversial content that would define the Attitude Era. The Curtain Call incident and the departure of Razor Ramon and Diesel to WCW (where they would form the nWo) marked the end of an era and the beginning of the Monday Night Wars.
