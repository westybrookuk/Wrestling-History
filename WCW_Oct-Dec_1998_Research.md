# WCW October-December 1998 Research Report

**PERIOD:** October 1, 1998 - December 31, 1998

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This period marked the end of Goldberg's legendary undefeated streak and the beginning of WCW's decline. Kevin Nash ended Goldberg's 173-0 streak at Starrcade with Scott Hall's cattle prod interference, then immediately lost the title to Hulk Hogan in the infamous "Fingerpoke of Doom" on January 4, 1999. The nWo civil war continued, the Ultimate Warrior returned for a disastrous feud with Hogan, and WCW's creative direction became increasingly erratic.

## INJURIES / BACKSTAGE INCIDENTS / CONTROVERSIES

### Goldberg's Streak Ends
- **Date:** December 27, 1998
- **Incident:** Goldberg lost his 173-0 undefeated streak to Kevin Nash at Starrcade
- **Details:** Scott Hall interfered disguised as security, shocked Goldberg with cattle prod, Nash hit Jackknife Powerbomb
- **Impact:** Ended one of the most famous streaks in wrestling history
- **Controversy:** The finish was heavily criticized; many felt it was poorly executed

### Hogan vs. Warrior Debacle
- **Date:** October 25, 1998
- **Incident:** Hollywood Hogan vs. The Ultimate Warrior at Halloween Havoc
- **Details:** Match was universally panned as one of the worst in WCW history
- **Impact:** Eric Bischoff later admitted the match "stunk up the joint"
- **Controversy:** WCW built a feud between two guys who had their biggest match in WWF 8 years earlier

### The Fingerpoke of Doom Setup
- **Date:** December 27, 1998
- **Incident:** Kevin Nash won the World Title from Goldberg at Starrcade
- **Details:** Nash would lose the title to Hulk Hogan on January 4, 1999 in the infamous "Fingerpoke of Doom"
- **Impact:** This sequence effectively killed the nWo, the Wolfpac, and Goldberg's momentum all at once
- **Controversy:** Considered one of the biggest creative mistakes in wrestling history

### Rick Steiner vs. Scott Steiner
- **Date:** October 25, 1998
- **Incident:** Brothers faced off at Halloween Havoc
- **Details:** Rick Steiner pinned Scott to win the tag titles with Buff Bagwell
- **Impact:** Continued the Steiner brothers feud
- **Controversy:** The brothers' real-life tension was worked into the angle

### Eric Bischoff vs. Ric Flair
- **Date:** December 27, 1998
- **Incident:** Bischoff defeated Flair at Starrcade
- **Details:** WCW Vice President Eric Bischoff wrestled and defeated "Nature Boy" Ric Flair
- **Impact:** Showed how far WCW's creative had gone off the rails
- **Controversy:** A non-wrestler executive beating a wrestling legend was seen as absurd

## GAME EFFECT SUGGESTIONS

### Momentum Effects
- **Goldberg:** Massive negative momentum after losing streak; however, still extremely over with fans
- **Kevin Nash:** Massive positive momentum from winning World Title and ending streak
- **Hollywood Hogan:** Positive momentum from Warrior feud victory, but the match quality was terrible
- **The Ultimate Warrior:** Negative momentum; the return and Hogan feud were poorly received
- **Diamond Dallas Page:** Positive momentum from good performance against Goldberg
- **Rick Steiner:** Positive momentum from winning tag titles and beating his brother
- **Scott Steiner:** Negative momentum from losing to his brother
- **Kevin Nash:** Massive negative momentum after immediately losing title to Hogan (Fingerpoke of Doom)
- **Hulk Hogan:** Massive negative momentum from Fingerpoke of Doom; effectively killed his credibility

### Fan Trust Effects
- **Goldberg's streak ending:** Negative - fans invested in the streak were disappointed
- **Hogan vs. Warrior match:** Very negative - universally panned as terrible
- **Fingerpoke of Doom:** Extremely negative - killed nWo, Wolfpac, and Goldberg momentum simultaneously
- **Bischoff beating Flair:** Negative - seen as absurd and out of touch
- **Nash winning then losing title:** Extremely negative - made the streak ending meaningless

### Financial Effects
- **Halloween Havoc buyrate:** 0.78 - below average for WCW PPV
- **World War 3 attendance:** 17,670 - solid but not spectacular
- **Starrcade buyrate:** 460,000 - good buyrate but the show was critically panned
- **Warrior return:** Initially positive for ticket sales, but the poor match quality hurt long-term interest

### Title Prestige Effects
- **World Title:** Very negative - Nash winning then immediately losing to Hogan made the streak ending meaningless
- **Tag Titles:** Neutral - Rick Steiner & Buff Bagwell were credible champions
- **US Title:** Neutral - Bret Hart was a credible champion
- **TV Title:** Neutral - Konnan was a credible champion
- **Cruiserweight Title:** Positive - Billy Kidman was over and the division was strong

### Morale Effects
- **Goldberg:** Negative - worked stiff by Nash, frustrated with how his streak ended
- **Kevin Nash:** Negative - pressured by Hogan to lose the title immediately
- **Hollywood Hogan:** Positive - got the victory over Warrior and the title from Nash
- **The Ultimate Warrior:** Very negative - the return was a disaster
- **nWo members:** Very negative - the faction was effectively killed by the Fingerpoke
- **Wolfpac members:** Very negative - their leader (Nash) was forced to lose the title

### Booking Effects
- **Goldberg's streak ending:** Should have been done differently; having Nash win then lose immediately was a major mistake
- **Hogan vs. Warrior:** Should never have happened; the match was terrible and WCW knew it
- **Fingerpoke of Doom:** Should never have happened; killed multiple over acts at once
- **Bischoff vs. Flair:** Should never have happened; showed creative was out of touch
- **nWo civil war resolution:** Should have been done through proper wrestling angles, not the Fingerpoke

### Long-term Consequences
- **WCW's decline accelerated:** The Fingerpoke of Doom effectively killed WCW's momentum
- **Goldberg's push stalled:** He wouldn't regain the World Title until 2000
- **nWo effectively ended:** The faction never recovered from the Fingerpoke
- **Fan trust eroded:** Fans realized WCW was willing to undo years of storytelling in one night
- **Creative credibility damaged:** The booking decisions showed WCW was rudderless

## CONCLUSION

October-December 1998 was a pivotal period that marked the beginning of WCW's terminal decline. While Goldberg's streak ending was a major moment, the way it was handled - with Nash winning then immediately losing to Hogan in the Fingerpoke of Doom - effectively killed multiple over acts and storylines at once. The Ultimate Warrior's disastrous return, Eric Bischoff's absurd victory over Ric Flair, and the general creative missteps showed that WCW had lost its way. The nWo, which had carried WCW for two years, was effectively dead. Goldberg's momentum was stalled. Fan trust was eroding rapidly. This period set the stage for WCW's rapid decline in 1999 and eventual sale to WWF in 2001.
