# WWF January-March 1995 Research Report

**Period Covered:** January 1, 1995 - March 31, 1995

## Executive Summary

The January-March 1995 period marked the beginning of WWF's "New Generation" era following Hulk Hogan's departure to WCW in 1994. Diesel (Kevin Nash) was positioned as the new face of the company and WWF Champion, having defeated Bob Backlund at Survivor Series 1994. The period featured Royal Rumble 1995 (January 22, Tampa), where Shawn Michaels won the Rumble and Jeff Jarrett won the Intercontinental Title from Razor Ramon. The period built toward WrestleMania XI (April 2, Hartford), which featured Lawrence Taylor vs. Bam Bam Bigelow as the main event. WWF launched the "In Your House" series of lower-priced monthly PPVs in response to WCW increasing their PPV schedule. Diesel's championship reign was met with mixed reactions, as fans and critics questioned whether he could fill Hogan's shoes. The period saw continued feuds between Bret and Owen Hart, the rise of new talent like Shawn Michaels, and celebrity involvement with Lawrence Taylor and Pamela Anderson.

## Build to WrestleMania XI

**Background:**
- At Royal Rumble, Bam Bam Bigelow was pinned in the Tag Team Title Tournament Final
- Fans heckled Bigelow after the loss

## Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### Diesel as Hulk Hogan Replacement

**Incident:** Vince McMahon pushed Diesel as the new face of WWF to replace Hulk Hogan

**Details:**
- Diesel had been pushed aggressively since mid-1994
- Won WWF Title on November 26, 1994 at Survivor Series
- McMahon wanted Diesel to be "the next Hulk Hogan"
- Diesel was given Hogan-like babyface promos and character traits
- Fans were divided on whether Diesel could fill Hogan's shoes
- Critics noted Diesel was more effective as a heel (1-2-3 Kid's bodyguard)
- Diesel's reign as champion was described as "D" grade by some retro reviewers
- The push was seen as forced by many fans
- **Source:** https://www.wrestling20yrs.com/blog/why-did-diesel-fail-as-wwf-champion-in-1995

### The "New Generation" Era Begins

**Incident:** WWF rebranded as "New Generation" after Hogan and other stars left for WCW

**Details:**
- Hulk Hogan left for WCW in 1994
- Randy Savage, Ultimate Warrior, and others had already left
- WWF needed new stars to carry the company
- Diesel, Shawn Michaels, Bret Hart, Razor Ramon, Undertaker were pushed as new faces
- The era is widely seen as the nadir of WWF creatively and commercially
- Fans missed the bigger-than-life characters of the 1980s
- The product was seen as lacking star power
- **Source:** https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki.php/YMMV/WWENewGenerationEra

- This was controversial as it wasn't a title match

## Conclusion

The January-March 1995 period marked the beginning of WWF's "New Generation" era without Hulk Hogan. Diesel was positioned as the new face and WWF Champion, but the push was met with mixed reactions. Royal Rumble 1995 featured Jeff Jarrett winning the Intercontinental Title, The 1-2-3 Kid & Bob Holly winning the Tag Team Titles, and Shawn Michaels winning the Royal Rumble. The period built toward WrestleMania XI (April 2), which would feature Lawrence Taylor vs. Bam Bam Bigelow as the controversial main event. WWF launched the "In Your House" series to provide more monthly PPV content at lower prices. The period highlighted the challenges of replacing Hogan and the creative difficulties of the New Generation era, with multiple babyfaces and a lack of compelling heel factions. Despite these challenges, WWF continued to produce content and build toward major events, with Shawn Michaels emerging as a top star and Bret Hart continuing his technical excellence.

## Simulator-Use Notes

### Title Lineage Implications
- **WWF Title (Diesel):** Diesel's reign began at Survivor Series 1994 and continued through this period. The Royal Rumble match with Bret Hart ended in a double countout - this should be modeled as a non-decisive finish that doesn't significantly impact title prestige but does create booking difficulties (two babyfaces).
- **IC Title (Jeff Jarrett):** Jarrett won his first IC Title at Royal Rumble. This should be tracked as a career milestone. The title changed hands multiple times on house shows in April-May 1995 (documented in WWF Apr-Jun 1995 report) - this rapid turnover devalued the championship.
