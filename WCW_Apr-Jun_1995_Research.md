# WCW Research Report: April 1995 – End of June 1995

> **Note re: continuity with Jan–Mar 1995 report:** At the end of March 1995, the titleholders were:
> - WCW World Heavyweight: **Hulk Hogan** (held since 7/17/1994)
> - WCW US Heavyweight: **Vader** (stripped late April; see below)
> - WCW World Tag Team: **Harlem Heat** (Booker T & Stevie Ray; won at Clash XXX taping 1/8/95)
> - WCW World Television: **Arn Anderson**
>
> All confirmed changes below update or supersede those assumptions.

---

## 1. Title Changes

### 1A. WCW United States Heavyweight Championship — VACATED (Vader stripped)

- **Date:** April 23, 1995
- **Championship:** WCW United States Heavyweight Championship
- **What happened:** Vader was officially stripped of the US Title by WCW Commissioner Nick Bockwinkel for "multiple violent offenses." The storyline catalyst was Vader and Ric Flair's attack on Dave Sullivan (Evad Sullivan). Bockwinkel announced the stripping on WCW Saturday Night (taped April 12, aired April 22). A 16-man tournament was ordered to crown a new champion.
- **Taping date:** April 12, 1995 (Center Stage Theatre, Atlanta, GA)
- **Air date:** April 22, 1995 (WCW Saturday Night)
- **Source:**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Van_Vader ("Vader was stripped of the United States Heavyweight Championship on April 23, 1995, for multiple violent offenses")
  - https://thehistoryofwwe.com/wcw-results-1995/ (Bockwinkel announcement)
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_American_Bash_(1995) ("he was stripped of the United States Championship for repeatedly attacking and injuring his fellow competitors, the last of which was Evad Sullivan")
- **[UNCONFIRMED — FLAG]:** WWE.com's title history page says "WCW stripped Vader of the title in March 1995" (https://www.wwe.com/classics/titlehistory/unitedstates/30445413211), which contradicts the April 23 date cited by Wikipedia and prowestlinghistory.com. The April 22 air date of the announcement on Saturday Night and the tournament starting that same night make April 23 most likely correct for the formal stripping. The WWE.com reference may be imprecise or referring to the Dave Sullivan attack itself (which occurred in March).

- **Air date:** April 22, 1995 (WCW Saturday Night)

### 2D. Collision in Korea — April 28–29, 1995

- **Event dates:** April 28 and April 29, 1995
- **Air date (North America):** August 4, 1995 (PPV)
- **Show:** WCW/NJPW Collision in Korea (Pyongyang International Sports and Culture Festival for Peace)
- **Venue:** May Day Stadium, Pyongyang, North Korea
- **Attendance:** Day 1 ~150,000; Day 2 ~165,000 (combined ~315,000 — largest wrestling event in history; numbers disputed)
- **Promotions:** WCW / NJPW (joint)
- **WCW talent involved:**
  - Ric Flair (lost to Antonio Inoki in the Day 2 main event)
  - The Steiner Brothers (Rick & Scott Steiner) (def. Hiroshi Hase & Kensuke Sasaki, Day 2)
  - Road Warrior Hawk (def. Tadao Yasuda, Day 2)
  - Chris Benoit / Wild Pegasus (def. 2 Cold Scorpio, Day 2; lost to Hase, Day 1)
  - Scott Norton (drew with IWGP Champion Shinya Hashimoto, Day 1)
  - 2 Cold Scorpio (lost to Pegasus, Day 2; def. Shinjiro Otani, Day 1)
- **Road Warrior Animal:** Out with a back injury; did not travel. Hawk competed as a singles wrestler.
- **Why it mattered:**
  - First American wrestling promotion event in North Korea
  - Largest wrestling attendance ever recorded
  - Ric Flair took the main event loss to Inoki — Flair was chosen because Hogan declined to participate
  - WCW wrestlers were essentially on an NJPW tour bonus; paid standard rates, no extra bonus
  - Behind-the-scenes chaos: fight between 2 Cold Scorpio and Road Warrior Hawk
  - Bizarre political context; later used in North Korean propaganda

## 4. Roster Moves / Debuts / Exits

### 4A. Vader — Stripped of US Title and Effectively Written Off Main Scene

- **Date:** April 23, 1995 (formal stripping)
- **Person:** Big Van Vader (Leon White)
- **Change:** Stripped of US Title for "multiple violent offenses" (attacking Dave Sullivan, etc.). Was also indefinitely suspended from WCW shortly after for a backstage altercation with Paul Orndorff (exact date uncertain; occurred around July-August 1995 at a house show). During April–June, Vader remained active on TV and at Slamboree/Great American Bash but was being phased out of the US Title picture.
- **Impact:** Vader remained on TV as a top heel but without a title. He was paired with Flair against Hogan/Savage at Slamboree (lost). The Dungeon of Doom was being built separately and Vader was being positioned as a "hired gun" for them.
- **Confidence:** High for stripping; medium for exact date of Orndorff altercation.
- **Source:**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Van_Vader
  - https://prowrestling.fandom.com/wiki/Big_Van_Vader

### 4B. The Giant (Paul Wight) — Teased, Not Yet Debuted

- **Date:** May 21, 1995 (first visual tease)
- **Person:** Paul Wight (billed as "The Giant")
- **Change:** First spotted standing near the entrance curtain during Hogan & Savage's entrance at Slamboree. A large unknown man. Vignettes for the Dungeon of Doom (which would feature The Giant) had been filmed by late May — 14 vignettes were reportedly shot.
- **Impact:** Building massive anticipation. The Giant would not make his in-ring debut until Halloween Havoc (October 29, 1995).
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slamboree_(1995) ("an unknown man can be seen standing near the entryway (later revealed to be The Giant)")
  - https://www.blogofdoom.com/2019/04/26/what-the-world-was-watching-wcw-saturday-night-may-27-1995/ ("Paul Wight, who will be billed as the Giant, will be part of the group")

### 4C. Steve Austin — Triceps Injury in Japan, Effective May 27, 1995

- **Date:** May 27, 1995
- **Person:** "Stunning" Steve Austin
- **Change:** Suffered a torn right triceps during a tag team match on a NJPW tour (May 27, 1995 — third night), working alongside Arn Anderson against Shinya Hashimoto and Manabu Nakanishi. Austin landed wrong on his arm after a splash and gutted out the rest of the tour. He underwent surgery upon return to the US and was projected to be out for several months.
- **Impact:** This ended planned Hollywood Blonds reunion with Brian Pillman. It also removed Austin from WCW TV during a key building period. He would be fired by Eric Bischoff on September 15, 1995, while still injured. He appeared at ECW shortly after before signing with WWF in late 1995.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://cultaholic.com/posts/steve-austins-wcw-run---what-went-wrong ("On the third night of the tour on May 27, 1995, however, during a match alongside Anderson against Shinya Hashimoto and Manabu Nakanishi, Austin tore his right triceps")
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Cold_Steve_Austin ("During the NJPW tour, Austin suffered a torn triceps")
  - https://www.reddit.com/r/TheDirtsheets/comments/3s36ch/ (Wrestling Observer July-Dec 1995 excerpts by Dave Meltzer)

### 4D. Ric Flair — Part-Time Booker Quit/Returned (April 1995)

- **Date:** Early April 1995
- **Person:** Ric Flair
- **Change:** Flair quit as booker in early April after Hogan refused to work the final two weeks of TV tapings prior to Uncensored. Flair returned to the booking role a few days later. Meanwhile, Macho Man also tried to get creative control of his own programs.
- **Impact:** This booking instability contributed to creative chaos. Kevin Sullivan would eventually take over as lead booker.
- **Confidence:** High (per culturecrossfire.com Slamboree recap, which cites contemporary reports).
- **Source:**
  - https://culturecrossfire.com/wrestling/wcw-slamboree-95/ ("Ric Flair quit as booker in early April after Hogan refused to work the final two weeks of TV tapings prior to Uncensored and Macho Man tried to get creative control of his own programs too. Flair came back to the job a few days later")

### 4E. Dungeon of Doom — Formation Announced (Vignettes Filmed May 1995)

- **Date:** By May 27, 1995 (vignettes already filmed)
- **People involved:** Kevin Sullivan (Taskmaster), The Master (King Curtis Iaukea), The Giant (Paul Wight), The Shark (Avalanche/John Tenta, renamed), The Zodiac (The Butcher/Ed Leslie, renamed), Kamala, Meng
- **Change:** WCW was "all-in on the new Dungeon of Doom faction." Fourteen vignettes had been filmed to hype the new faction and its feud with Hulk Hogan. The group was being built around The Giant as the ultimate weapon.
- **Impact:** The Dungeon would become WCW's primary heel faction through late 1995 and into 1996. It was Kevin Sullivan's creative baby, designed to build The Giant over as Hogan's ultimate challenger.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://www.blogofdoom.com/2019/04/26/what-the-world-was-watching-wcw-saturday-night-may-27-1995/ ("WCW is all-in on the new Dungeon of Doom faction that will be headed by King Curtis Iaukea and Kevin Sullivan. Paul Wight, who will be billed as the Giant, will be part of the group along with Avalanche, who will be christened as 'the Great White Shark.'")

### 4F. Four Horsemen — Teased Reformation (April–June 1995)

- **Date:** April–May 1995 (ongoing tease)
- **People involved:** Ric Flair, Arn Anderson, Steve Austin (injured before formal reunion)
- **Change:** By March 1995, rumours surfaced that a new Four Horsemen would form with Flair, Arn Anderson, and Steve Austin filling the "Tully Blanchard" role. Austin's triceps injury on May 27 killed this plan. The Horsemen wouldn't formally re-form until September 1995 (with Brian Pillman and eventually Chris Benoit replacing Austin).
- **Impact:** The failed Horsemen reunion with Austin was a significant missed opportunity. Austin later said Sullivan killed the plan. The eventual reformation with Pillman/Benoit was less impactful.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://cultaholic.com/posts/steve-austins-wcw-run---what-went-wrong ("By March 1995, rumours surfaced that in Flair's planned comeback to the ring, there would be a new incarnation of The Four Horsemen...   - https://www.blogofdoom.com/2019/05/10/what-the-world-was-watching-wcw-main-event-june-4-1995/ ("The Flair, Anderson, and Austin team is a nice snapshot in time of a Four Horsemen that never was")

### 4G. Several Workers Cut / Told They Were Being Released

- **Date:** ~April–May 1995
- **People:** Joey Maggs, Steve Keirn, Brad Armstrong, Steve Austin (initially), Brutus Beefcake
- **Change:** Multiple workers were told they were being cut in cost-cutting moves. Brutus Beefcake was reportedly shocked. Austin and Beefcake were saved after Flair and Hogan went to bat for them — but Austin then got injured in Japan and was ultimately fired in September anyway.
- **Impact:** Reflected Ted Turner's cost-cutting mandates as he sought to buy more TV stations. Jim Barnett and other WCW officials were also fired. Jerry Jarrett was signed as a consultant, with USWA becoming a WCW training ground.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**

  - https://culturecrossfire.com/wrestling/wcw-slamboree-95/ ("Legendary promoter Jim Barnett and several other WCW officials were fired in cost cutting moves")

### 4H. Gordon Solie — Still Active (Last Broadcast July 1, 1995)

- **Date:** June 1995 (still on air)
- **Person:** Gordon Solie (legendary commentator)
- **Change:** Solie was still providing commentary on WCW Pro during this period. His final WCW broadcast would be July 1, 1995 (WCW Pro) — technically just outside this window but worth noting as a transition was imminent.

## 5. Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### 5A. Ric Flair & Vader Attack Dave Sullivan (March/April 1995)

- **Date:** Late March / early April 1995 (exact date uncertain)
- **People involved:** Big Van Vader, Ric Flair, Dave Sullivan
- **What happened:** Vader and Flair brutally attacked Dave Sullivan, injuring him. This was the "last straw" that led to Bockwinkel stripping Vader of the US Title. Sullivan was shown "recovering in hospital" on the April 15 Saturday Night episode, during which Diamond Doll visited him and developed an affection for him (leading to the DDP vs. Sullivan arm wrestling angle at Great American Bash).
- **Why it mattered:** This attack was the direct catalyst for the US Title being vacated and the tournament. It also launched the DDP vs. Dave Sullivan feud (Diamond Doll angle).
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_American_Bash_(1995) ("he was stripped of the United States Championship for repeatedly attacking and injuring his fellow competitors, the last of which was Evad Sullivan")
  - https://culturecrossfire.com/wrestling/wcw-slamboree-95/ ("Vader was stripped of the US title after he and Flair brutally attacked Dave Sullivan")

### 5B. Tony Schiavone — Neck Surgery (May 1995)

- **Date:** ~May 1995
- **Person:** Tony Schiavone
- **What happened:** Schiavone took time off to undergo neck surgery. Eric Bischoff filled in on commentary for Slamboree.
- **Why it mattered:** WCW's lead commentator was sidelined. Bischoff handled Slamboree commentary.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slamboree_(1995) ("Eric Bischoff filled in on commentary for Tony Schiavone at this event; Schiavone took time off to undergo neck surgery")

### 5C. Renegade Tassels Removed / WWF Legal Notice

- **Date:** ~May–June 1995
- **People involved:** The Renegade (Rick Wilson), WWF, WCW
- **What happened:** WWF sent a legal notice to WCW over the Renegade gimmick stealing The Ultimate Warrior's look. The Renegade had his tassels removed and was told to stop shaking the ropes. His promos were also described as disasters, requiring up to two dozen takes with constant line reductions.
- **Why it mattered:** Demonstrated WCW's willingness to use blatant ripoffs and the legal/reputational consequences. The Renegade's push would begin cratering shortly after the Great American Bash title win.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://culturecrossfire.com/wrestling/wcw-slamboree-95/ ("The Renegade had his tassels removed and was told to stop shaking the ropes after the WWF sent a legal notice over WCW stealing the Ultimate Warrior's gimmick. Renegade has been struggling to even work 30 seconds squash matches without blowing spots. His promos have also been a disaster with it taking up to two dozen takes and constant reduction in lines to get something usable from him.")
- **[FLAG]:** The exact date of the WWF notice is uncertain. The culturecrossfire recap dates it to around the Slamboree period but it may have been slightly before or after.

### 5D. 2 Cold Scorpio vs. Road Warrior Hawk Fight (Collision in Korea)

- **Date:** April 28–29, 1995 (North Korea)
- **People involved:** 2 Cold Scorpio, Road Warrior Hawk
- **What happened:** During the Collision in Korea tour in North Korea, a backstage fight broke out between 2 Cold Scorpio and Road Warrior Hawk. The altercation reportedly continued even after leaving North Korea.

## 6. Game Effect Suggestions

### Momentum
- **Hogan:** Still World Champion but stagnating. Notably ABSENT from Great American Bash card — a deliberate creative decision to build toward "the ultimate surprise" / Renegade. The Hogan-centric booking was wearing thin.
- **Sting:** RISING. Won the US Title at Great American Bash. Strong showing throughout. Positioned as WCW's #1 babyface below Hogan.
- **Vader:** DECLINING. Stripped of US Title, never beat Hogan cleanly, being sidelined. Would eventually be written out entirely.
- **Savage:** Stable. Losing to Flair at Great American Bash in a personal feud. Poffo angle added heat.
- **Ric Flair:** RISING as a heel. Active wrestler, won the tournament semifinal (by DQ/no contest), beat Savage at Great American Bash. Building toward Four Horsemen reunion.
- **Renegade:** INITIAL POP followed by CRASH. Won TV Title at Great American Bash but was widely criticized. WWF legal issues would kill the gimmick.
- **Nasty Boys:** UP. Won tag titles at Slamboree, lost them back to Harlem Heat within ~5 weeks. Active feud team.
- **Harlem Heat:** Stable. Lost titles at Slamboree but regained them quickly. Solid tag team mainstays.
- **The Giant:** BUILDING. Teased at Slamboree, vignettes filmed, not yet debuted. Massive anticipation.
- **Blue Bloods:** RISING. Strong showings, competitive tag title match at Great American Bash.

### Money
- **PPV buyrates declining sharply:**
  - Uncensored (March): 0.96
  - Slamboree (May): 0.57
  - Great American Bash (June): 0.51
- Hogan absent from Great American Bash likely hurt the buy significantly.
- Collision in Korea was a unique one-off PPV (aired August 4) — financial details uncertain.
- **WCW had not yet launched Monday Nitro** — still 3 months away.
- Live attendance was middling: 7,000 for Slamboree, 6,000 for Great American Bash.

### Morale
- **Fragile.** Flair quit as booker in early April, then returned. Multiple workers cut. Austin injured and heading toward firing. The Renegade gimmick embarrassing to many. Sting was the one guy who was consistently protected.
- Kevin Sullivan consolidating creative power.
- Jerry Jarrett signed as consultant; USWA as training ground.
- Cost-cutting (Jim Barnett and others fired).

### Network Health
- **TBS/WTBS:** Primary platform. Saturday Night, Worldwide, Pro were the main TV shows.
- **Monday Nitro still 3 months away** (September 4, 1995).
- No major new network deals for PPV.

### Fan Trust
- **Significantly eroding.**
  - Renegade as Warrior clone was a trust-killer.
  - PPV quality declining (0.51 buyrate for Great American Bash).
  - Hogan never losing cleanly created audience apathy.
  - Uncensored's "no DQ" concept undermined by Savage match ending in DQ.
  - US Title being treated as a prop (Vader barely defended it, then stripped).
  - Some positives: Sting winning the US Title was well-received. Nasty Boys vs. Harlem Heat was a solid tag feud.

### Title Prestige
- **World Title (Hogan):** DECLINING. Never lost cleanly. DQ finishes.
- **US Title:** RESET. Vacated, tournament won by Sting. Sting's reign restored some credibility.
- **TV Title:** CRATERED. Renegade beating Arn Anderson was widely seen as the belt hitting its lowest point.
- **Tag Titles:** Active. Nasty Boys ↔ Harlem Heat feud was solid and kept the titles relevant.

### Roster Availability
- **Hogan:** Available, main eventing PPVs
- **Savage:** Available, co-main events
- **Sting:** Available, now US Champion
- **Vader:** Available but being phased down
- **Ric Flair:** Active, prominent heel
- **Arn Anderson:** Active, lost TV Title
- **Steve Austin:** INJURED (torn triceps, out from May 27). Would be fired in September.
- **Brian Pillman:** Active
- **Road Warrior Hawk:** Active (singles). **Animal still out with back injury.**
- **The Giant:** NOT YET DEBUTED (teased at Slamboree, vignettes filmed). Would debut in-ring at Halloween Havoc (October 29).
- **Diamond Dallas Page:** Active, mid-card heel. Kimberly (Diamond Doll) valet active.
- **Chris Benoit (Wild Pegasus):** Active (Collision in Korea, TV tapings)
- **Eddie Guerrero:** Active
- **Harlem Heat:** Active, tag champions (regained by late June)
- **Nasty Boys:** Active, lost titles by late June
- **Blue Bloods:** Active, rising
- **Dean Malenko:** Active (not in this specific window on major TV but on the circuit)
- **Meng:** Active, Col. Parker's man
- **Big Bubba Rogers:** Active
- **Blacktop Bully:** Active
- **Jim Duggan:** Active
- **Dustin Rhodes:** Active

1. **Vader US Title stripping:** The Jan–Mar report noted this would happen "around April 23, 1995." Confirmed: April 23, 1995 is the date cited by Wikipedia and others. WWE.com says "March 1995" which appears to be imprecise or refer to the Dave Sullivan attack itself.
2. **Harlem Heat tag title reign:** Jan–Mar report confirmed they won titles on January 8 (aired January 21). This report confirms they held them through Slamboree (May 21), where they lost to the Nasty Boys. No conflict.

4. **Steve Austin:** Jan–Mar report noted his return from knee injury in February 1995. This report adds the triceps injury (May 27, 1995) that ultimately led to his firing. Consistent.
5. **The Giant:** Jan–Mar report noted he had not yet debuted. This report confirms the first visual tease at Slamboree (May 21). Consistent.
