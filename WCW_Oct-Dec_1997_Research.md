### 1E. WCW World Heavyweight Championship — Hollywood Hogan → Sting (Controversial)

- **Date:** December 28, 1997
- **Championship:** WCW World Heavyweight Championship
- **Winner:** Sting
- **Loser:** "Hollywood" Hogan (c) (nWo)
- **Show:** WCW Starrcade '97 (PPV)
- **PPV date:** December 28, 1997
- **Venue:** MCI Center, Washington, D.C.
- **Attendance:** 17,500
- **Buyrate:** 1.9 (~655,000 buys — biggest in WCW history at the time)
- **Details:**
  - Sting submitted Hogan with the Scorpion Deathlock at approximately 12:53 (some sources say 13:20 or 22:54 — times vary by source).
  - **CONTROVERSIAL FINISH:** Referee Nick Patrick (nWo member) gave Hogan a legdrop and counted 1-2-3. The count was supposed to be a "fast count" (obviously fake) but Patrick counted at normal speed, making it appear Hogan actually won. **Bret Hart** (special guest referee for another match) stormed down, punched Patrick, took over as referee, and restarted the match. Sting then submitted Hogan cleanly.
  - Hogan's reign ended at approximately 406-470 days (sources vary on exact count, depending on whether the Luger 5-day reign is counted).
  - **THE NEXT NIGHT (Dec 29, Nitro):** There was a rematch. Patrick again gave Hogan a fast count. Referee Randy Anderson restarted. Sting won again.
  - **THEN:** The title was **vacated** in subsequent programming due to the controversial finishes. Sting won it back at SuperBrawl VIII (February 22, 1998).
  - **For implementation purposes:** Sting was recognized as champion from Dec 28 through the vacating (which happened in early January 1998 programming).
- **Why it mattered:**
  - This was the culmination of an 18-month storyline — Sting vs. Hogan, the entire nWo invasion angle.
  - The biggest buyrate in WCW history at the time.
  - The finish was botched and controversial — fans were confused and angry.
  - Sting's first televised match in over a year.
  - Bret Hart's involvement as the "savior" who restarted the match established him as a face.
  - Hogan later claimed he was screwed by Hart (playing the victim), when the actual plan was for Patrick to do a fast count.

### 2C. Bret Hart Debuts — December 15, 1997

- **Air date:** December 15, 1997 (Nitro #118)
- **Person:** Bret "The Hitman" Hart
- **What happened:**
  - Bret Hart made his WCW debut on Nitro, 36 days after the **Montreal Screwjob** at WWF Survivor Series (November 9, 1997).
  - Eric Bischoff (nWo) announced Hart was joining the nWo.
  - Hart was announced as the **special guest referee** for the Bischoff vs. Zbyszko match at Starrcade (Dec 28).

- **Why it mattered:**
  - The biggest wrestling news of 1997 — the biggest star in WWF just left for WCW after being screwed.
  - Hart's involvement in Starrcade's controversial finish was poetic justice.
  - Hart would become one of WCW's top stars for the next few years.

## 4. Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### 4A. Starrcade Main Event Finish — Botched

- **Date:** December 28, 1997 (Starrcade)
- **What happened:** The plan was for Nick Patrick (nWo referee) to do a "fast count" on Hogan's legdrop, making it obviously fake. Sting would kick out, then win cleanly. But Patrick counted at NORMAL speed, making it appear Hogan actually won. Bret Hart stormed down, punched Patrick, took over as referee, and restarted the match. Sting then submitted Hogan.
- **Why it mattered:**
  - The finish was supposed to be clean but looked controversial.
  - Fans were confused — did Hogan actually win? Did Hart screw Hogan?
  - Hogan later played the victim, claiming Hart screwed him (when the plan was always for Sting to win).
  - The next night on Nitro, the same thing happened again — Patrick fast count, Anderson restart.
  - The title was then vacated.
  - Sting had to win it back at SuperBrawl VIII (Feb 22, 1998).
  - Per Sting: "I don't know what happened."
  - Per TJR Wrestling review: "That match was an example of what was wrong with WCW. They spent over a year building up this match, then they finally get there and they can't even book the right finish."
- **Confidence:** High.
