# WWF July-September 1996 Research Report

**Period Covered:** July 1, 1996 - September 30, 1996

## Executive Summary

The July-September 1996 period was a pivotal transitional phase in WWF, marked by the devastating impact of the nWo formation in WCW and WWF's desperate attempts to respond. The period featured three major PPV events: In Your House 9: International Incident (July 21, Vancouver), SummerSlam 1996 (August 18, Cleveland), and In Your House 10: Mind Games (September 22, Philadelphia). The most significant developments were the firing of Ultimate Warrior in July for no-showing events, the return of Sycho Sid as his replacement, the debut of Faarooq and the formation of the Nation of Domination, and Ahmed Johnson's reign as Intercontinental Champion ending due to legitimate kidney problems. The period was dominated by the Monday Night Wars, as WWF struggled to compete with WCW's nWo storyline that had begun in July with the departures of Scott Hall (Razor Ramon) and Kevin Nash (Diesel). WWF's response included the controversial "fake Diesel and Razor Ramon" angle in September, which was universally panned. Shawn Michaels continued his first WWF Championship reign, successfully defending against Vader at SummerSlam and Mankind at In Your House 10.

**Results:**

## Television Highlights

### WWF Monday Night Raw

**July 8, 1996 (Raw, Green Bay, WI)**
- WWF President Gorilla Monsoon announced The Ultimate Warrior's indefinite suspension for no-showing events in Indianapolis, Detroit, and Pittsburgh
- The Ultimate Warrior defeated Owen Hart by DQ in what would be his final televised WWF match
- Shawn Michaels and Ahmed Johnson secured Sycho Sid as their third man for In Your House 9
- **Source:** https://www.wrestleview.com/retro/387611-wwf-raw-results-july-8-1996-green-bay/

**July 22, 1996 (Raw)**
- WWF Intercontinental Champion Ahmed Johnson defeated WWF World Tag Team Champion Bart Gunn to retain the title
- Faarooq debuted and attacked Ahmed Johnson during a tag team match
- This was supposed to lead to a SummerSlam match between Ahmed Johnson and Faarooq for the IC title
- **Source:** https://darrenswinloss.wordpress.com/raw96/

**August 5, 1996 (Raw)**
- Ahmed Johnson won an 11-man battle royal to become #1 contender for the WWF Championship
- Participants included The Undertaker, Mankind, Goldust, Owen Hart, British Bulldog, Steve Austin, Savio Vega, Justin Bradshaw, and Marc Mero
- Johnson was diagnosed with legitimate kidney problems after this match and forced to vacate the IC title
- **Source:** https://www.onlineworldofwrestling.com/profile/ahmed-johnson/

**September 23, 1996 (Raw)**
- Faarooq defeated Savio Vega in a King of the Ring Tournament match
- This was part of the build to the Intercontinental Championship tournament
- **Source:** https://darrenswinloss.wordpress.com/raw96/

**September 30, 1996 (Raw, America On-line special)**
- Jim Ross announced that "Diesel and Razor Ramon are on their way back to the WWF"
- This was a desperate attempt to counter WCW's nWo storyline
- The announcement was a hoax - Rick Bognar appeared as "Fake Razor Ramon" and Glen Jacobs (future Kane) appeared as "Fake Diesel"

## Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### Ultimate Warrior's Firing

**Incident:** Ultimate Warrior fired for no-showing events

**Details:**
- Date: July 8, 1996 (suspended), officially fired shortly after
- Warrior no-showed events in Indianapolis, Detroit, and Pittsburgh
- He claimed he was grieving his father's death, but McMahon discovered Warrior had told him years earlier that he hated his father and had gone no-contact
- Warrior was also upset that WWF used his "Always Believe" logo at a toy convention without his permission
- McMahon fired Warrior and released him from his contract
- This was Warrior's final WWF appearance until his Hall of Fame induction in 2014
- **Source:** https://www.wrestlinginc.com/1140150/a-ravaged-warriors-defeat-looking-back-at-ultimate-warriors-failed-wwe-return-in-1996/

### Ahmed Johnson's Kidney Problems

**Incident:** Ahmed Johnson diagnosed with legitimate kidney problems

**Details:**
- Date: August 1996
- Johnson was attacked by Faarooq on July 22, which was supposed to lead to a SummerSlam match
- However, Johnson was diagnosed with legitimate kidney problems and forced to vacate the Intercontinental Championship
- The attack was turned into a storyline to explain his absence
- Johnson would return later but his injuries prevented him from reaching main event status
- **Source:** https://prowrestling.fandom.com/wiki/Ahmed_Johnson

### Paul Bearer's Betrayal of Undertaker

**Incident:** Paul Bearer turned on Undertaker and sided with Mankind

**Details:**
- Date: August 18, 1996 (SummerSlam)
- During the Boiler Room Brawl between Undertaker and Mankind, Paul Bearer refused to hand Undertaker the urn
- Bearer then sided with Mankind, allowing Mankind to win the match
- This was a major storyline development, beginning Mankind's association with "Uncle Paul" Bearer
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SummerSlam_(1996)

### Fake Diesel and Razor Ramon Debacle

**Incident:** WWF attempted to replace departed stars with fake versions

**Details:**
- Date: September 30, 1996
- After Scott Hall (Razor Ramon) and Kevin Nash (Diesel) left for WCW in May 1996 and formed the nWo, WWF attempted to counter by introducing fake versions
- Rick Bognar appeared as "Fake Razor Ramon" and Glen Jacobs (future Kane) appeared as "Fake Diesel"
- Jim Ross made the announcement, building it up as a major return
- The angle was universally panned and quickly dropped
- Ross was made the scapegoat for the angle
- This was seen as a desperate attempt to compete with WCW's nWo storyline
- **Source:** https://ringthedamnbell.com/bait-and-switch-diesel-and-razor-ramon-return-to-the-wwf-in-1996/

### nWo Formation and Monday Night Wars Impact

**Incident:** Scott Hall and Kevin Nash left WWF for WCW, forming the nWo

**Details:**
- Date: May 1996 (departures), July 1996 (nWo formation with Hulk Hogan)
- Scott Hall (Razor Ramon) and Kevin Nash (Diesel) left WWF for WCW
- They formed the Outsiders, which became the nWo when Hulk Hogan turned heel
- The nWo storyline was a massive ratings success for WCW
- WWF struggled to compete, leading to desperate measures like the fake Diesel/Razor angle
- This marked a turning point in the Monday Night Wars, with WCW gaining the advantage
- **Source:** https://www.thesportster.com/wwe-scott-hall-departure-1996/

## Conclusion

The July-September 1996 period was a pivotal transitional phase in WWF, marked by the devastating impact of the nWo formation in WCW and WWF's desperate attempts to respond. The period featured three major PPV events, with the most significant being SummerSlam 1996, where Paul Bearer betrayed Undertaker and Shawn Michaels retained the WWF Championship against Vader. The period was dominated by the Monday Night Wars, as WWF struggled to compete with WCW's nWo storyline. The firing of Ultimate Warrior, the return of Sycho Sid, the debut of Faarooq and the Nation of Domination, and Ahmed Johnson's kidney problems were major developments. WWF's response to the nWo included the controversial "fake Diesel and Razor Ramon" angle, which was universally panned. This period marked a turning point in the Monday Night Wars, with WCW gaining the advantage and WWF struggling to find answers.
