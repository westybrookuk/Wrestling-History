## Executive Summary

The April-June 2004 period was dominated by Chris Benoit's historic double championship reign, Randy Orton's emergence as "The Legend Killer," and the culmination of the bitter Triple H vs. Shawn Michaels rivalry in a Hell in a Cell match at Bad Blood. This quarter featured three major PPV events: **Backlash** (April 18, Edmonton, Alberta), **Judgment Day** (May 16, Los Angeles, California), and **Bad Blood** (June 13, Columbus, Ohio).

**Key developments included:**
- **Chris Benoit** retained the World Heavyweight Championship at Backlash and Bad Blood, and became a double champion by winning the World Tag Team Championship with Edge
- **Randy Orton** fully embraced the "Legend Killer" persona, defeating Mick Foley in a bloody hardcore match at Backlash and spitting in Harley Race's face
- **Eddie Guerrero** retained the WWE Championship at Judgment Day despite losing to JBL by disqualification
- **Triple H** defeated Shawn Michaels in a 47-minute Hell in a Cell match at Bad Blood
- **JBL** emerged as the top contender for the WWE Championship, attacking Eddie Guerrero and establishing himself as a major heel
- **Trish Stratus** won the Women's Championship at Bad Blood in a Fatal Four-Way match

**PPV Statistics:**
- **Backlash:** 295,000 buys (terrible buyrate)
- **Judgment Day:** 18,722 attendance, 235,000 buys (bad buyrate)
- **Bad Blood:** 9,000 attendance, 290,000 buys (some sources say 264,000)

## Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### Randy Orton's "Legend Killer" Actions
Randy Orton fully embraced the "Legend Killer" persona during this period. Key incidents included:
- **April 26:** Orton spat in Harley Race's face on Raw, one of the most disrespectful acts in wrestling history
- **April 18:** Orton defeated Mick Foley in a bloody hardcore match at Backlash, involving thumbtacks and barbed wire
- Orton continued to disrespect wrestling legends throughout the quarter, establishing himself as one of the top heels in WWE

### Chris Benoit's Double Championship
Chris Benoit became a double champion on April 19, 2004, when he and Edge won the World Tag Team Championship from Ric Flair and Batista. Benoit held both the World Heavyweight Championship and World Tag Team Championship simultaneously, a rare feat in wrestling. He lost the tag team titles to La Résistance on May 31 but retained the World Heavyweight Championship.

### JBL's Heel Turn and Rise
John "Bradshaw" Layfield (JBL) turned heel at Judgment Day by attacking Eddie Guerrero and becoming the top contender for the WWE Championship. JBL was given the "Great American Award" by Kurt Angle, which made him the #1 contender. Guerrero stole the trophy and broke it, furthering the feud. JBL's "Clothesline from Hell" became his signature finisher during this period.

### Triple H vs. Shawn Michaels Hell in a Cell
The Triple H vs. Shawn Michaels rivalry culminated in a 47-minute Hell in a Cell match at Bad Blood. This was the third Hell in a Cell match between the two and is considered one of the best Hell in a Cell matches ever. Triple H won by hitting two Pedigrees.

### Shawn Michaels Suspension
On May 10, Shawn Michaels attacked Triple H during a match, resulting in Michaels being suspended by Eric Bischoff. This suspension kept Michaels off television for several weeks and added heat to the Triple H vs. Michaels feud.

### Kane's Interference
Kane interfered in multiple matches during this period, most notably in the World Tag Team Championship match at Bad Blood, where his interference caused Chris Benoit and Edge to lose the titles to La Résistance by disqualification. Kane also challenged Benoit for the World Heavyweight Championship at Bad Blood.

## Conclusion

The April-June 2004 period was defined by Chris Benoit's historic double championship reign, Randy Orton's emergence as "The Legend Killer," and the culmination of the Triple H vs. Shawn Michaels rivalry in a Hell in a Cell match at Bad Blood. The quarter opened with Backlash, where Benoit retained the World Heavyweight Championship in his hometown of Edmonton, and Orton defeated Mick Foley in a bloody hardcore match.

Judgment Day was highlighted by Eddie Guerrero's controversial retention of the WWE Championship against JBL by disqualification, and Chavo Guerrero's comedic win of the Cruiserweight Championship with one arm tied behind his back. JBL's heel turn and rise to top contender status was a major storyline development.

Bad Blood featured the critically acclaimed 47-minute Hell in a Cell match between Triple H and Shawn Michaels, which Triple H won. Chris Benoit retained the World Heavyweight Championship against Kane, and Trish Stratus won the Women's Championship in a Fatal Four-Way match.

The period also saw Chris Benoit become a double champion by winning the World Tag Team Championship with Edge on April 19, and Randy Orton's controversial "Legend Killer" actions, including spitting in Harley Race's face on April 26.

By the end of June 2004, the championship landscape was: Eddie Guerrero (WWE Champion, SmackDown!), Chris Benoit (World Heavyweight Champion, Raw), Randy Orton (Intercontinental Champion, Raw), La Résistance (World Tag Team Champions, Raw), Charlie Haas & Rico (WWE Tag Team Champions, SmackDown!), Trish Stratus (Women's Champion, Raw), Chavo Guerrero (Cruiserweight Champion, SmackDown!), and John Cena (United States Champion, SmackDown!).

The low PPV buyrates (Backlash: 295,000; Judgment Day: 235,000; Bad Blood: 290,000) were attributed to Benoit and Eddie Guerrero not being major draws as champions, and JBL being in the main event at Judgment Day before he was fully established as a top star.
