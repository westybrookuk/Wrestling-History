# WCW April-June 2000 Research Report

**Period Covered:** April 1, 2000 - June 30, 2000

## Executive Summary

The April-June 2000 period was the most chaotic and consequential in WCW's final year. On April 10, Vince Russo and Eric Bischoff returned to creative control and rebooted WCW by declaring all championships vacant, creating the infamous "New Blood vs. Millionaire's Club" angle. Spring Stampede 2000 served as the tournament to crown new champions, with the New Blood winning nearly all titles. The period saw the universally reviled decision to crown Hollywood actor David Arquette as WCW World Heavyweight Champion on April 25 (Thunder, aired April 26), a publicity stunt for the film *Ready to Rumble* that destroyed what remained of the title's credibility. The World Heavyweight Championship changed hands an astonishing 8 times during this three-month period, including Ric Flair winning and losing the title on the same night (May 29). WCW's ratings had collapsed to approximately 2.5-3.0 on Nitro by April, while WWF's Raw drew more than double that. The period included three PPVs (Spring Stampede, Slamboree, The Great American Bash) and ended with Jeff Jarrett having held the World Title for 41 days before Bash at the Beach in July.

## Title Changes

### WCW World Heavyweight Championship

**April 10, 2000 - Sid Vicious to Vacated**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Pepsi Center, Denver, CO)
- **Result:** Vince Russo & Eric Bischoff stripped all champions of their titles, rebooting WCW
- **Notes:** Sid Vicious initially refused to give up the title. Bischoff forced the issue by threatening to fire him. A tournament was announced to crown new champions at Spring Stampede. DDP won a 4-man tournament (Sting, Lex Luger, Sid) on this same night for the right to face Jarrett at Spring Stampede.
- **Source:** https://www.thewrestlinginsomniac.com/2017/05/the-year-wcw-championship-changed-hands.html

**April 16, 2000 - Vacated to Jeff Jarrett**
- **Event:** WCW Spring Stampede 2000 (United Center, Chicago, IL)
- **Match Type:** Tournament Final (Jeff Jarrett vs. Diamond Dallas Page)
- **Result:** Jeff Jarrett defeated Diamond Dallas Page (14:58)
- **Notes:** Kimberly Page turned on her husband DDP by hitting him with a guitar, allowing Jarrett to hit the Stroke and win the vacant WCW World Heavyweight Championship. This was Jarrett's first WCW World Heavyweight Championship. The New Blood won nearly all vacant titles at this event.
- **Attendance:** 12,556
- **PPV Buyrate:** 115,000 (0.25) - their highest buyrate in months, and the highest they would get for the rest of their existence
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Stampede_(2000)

**April 24, 2000 - Jeff Jarrett to Diamond Dallas Page**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Blue Cross Arena, Rochester, NY)
- **Match Type:** Steel Cage Match
- **Result:** Diamond Dallas Page defeated Jeff Jarrett (c) (6:18)
- **Notes:** After hitting the Diamond Cutter, Mike Awesome broke into the cage to stop referee Charles Robinson, but Chris Kanyon held him off, allowing Robinson to count to 3. David Arquette celebrated with Page and Kanyon after the match.
- **Attendance:** 7,713
- **Source:** https://thehistoryofwwe.com/wcw-monday-nitro-2000/

**April 25, 2000 - Diamond Dallas Page to David Arquette**
- **Event:** WCW Thunder (taped) / aired April 26, 2000 (War Memorial at Oncenter, Syracuse, NY)
- **Match Type:** Tag Team Match (DDP & David Arquette vs. Jeff Jarrett & Eric Bischoff)
- **Result:** David Arquette pinned Eric Bischoff to win the title
- **Notes:** The stipulation was that whoever got the pin would become WCW World Heavyweight Champion. Kimberly was the special guest referee. After Jarrett hit DDP with the championship belt, Arquette speared Bischoff. Despite Arquette not being the legal man, referee Mickey Jay counted Arquette's pinfall. This was a publicity stunt for the film *Ready to Rumble*. Tony Schiavone exclaimed "This is sports entertainment history that has never happened before!" while Mike Tenay asked "What does this mean for the future of WCW?!" Bobby Heenan was speechless. Arquette was vehemently against becoming champion but was pressured by Vince Russo. Arquette donated his WCW salary to the families of Brian Pillman and Owen Hart. This is widely regarded as one of the most embarrassing moments in wrestling history.
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Arquette

**May 7, 2000 - David Arquette to Jeff Jarrett**
- **Event:** WCW Slamboree 2000 (Kemper Arena, Kansas City, MO)
- **Match Type:** Triple Cage Match (Jeff Jarrett vs. David Arquette (c) vs. Diamond Dallas Page)
- **Result:** Jeff Jarrett won (15:29)
- **Notes:** Arquette turned on Page, hitting him with a guitar to allow Jarrett to win. After the match, Mike Awesome attacked DDP, and Kanyon tried to save Page but Awesome threw Kanyon off the second cage onto the entrance ramp, putting Kanyon's life at great risk. This was Jarrett's second WCW World Heavyweight Championship.
- **Attendance:** 7,165
- **PPV Buyrate:** 0.14
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slamboree_(2000)

**May 15, 2000 - Jeff Jarrett to Ric Flair**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Mississippi Coast Coliseum, Biloxi, MS)
- **Result:** Ric Flair defeated Jeff Jarrett (c) (6:17) with an inside cradle
- **Notes:** This was Flair's 15th World Championship reign. Vince Russo, wearing one of Flair's robes, David Flair, and Cruiserweight Champion Daffney were at ringside. Russo said "Holy shit" (bleeped) upon Flair's win. After the contest, Jarrett broke his guitar over referee Charles Robinson, and Jarrett, David Flair, and Russo triple-teamed Ric. As Russo tried to leave with the title belt, Kevin Nash hit a powerbomb on Jarrett, and Nash gave Ric the belt.
- **Attendance:** 8,550 (2,550 paid)
- **Source:** https://thehistoryofwwe.com/wcw-monday-nitro-2000/

**May 22, 2000 - Ric Flair to Vacated (stripped)**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Van Andel Arena, Grand Rapids, MI)
- **Result:** Vince Russo stripped Flair of the title
- **Notes:** On Thunder (May 23, aired May 24), Flair had defended his title in a handicap match defeating Jeff Jarrett, David Flair, Crowbar, and Daffney. After the match, Flair fell down leaving the ring with apparent equilibrium issues. It was later discovered to be an inner ear disorder requiring surgery, but prior to that discovery, WCW chose to vacate the title. Russo held a mock funeral for Flair's career. Kevin Nash popped out of a casket and stole the title.
- **Source:** https://www.thewrestlinginsomniac.com/2017/05/the-year-wcw-championship-changed-hands.html

**May 22, 2000 - Vacated to Jeff Jarrett**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Grand Rapids, MI)
- **Match Type:** Falls Count Anywhere Match
- **Result:** Jeff Jarrett defeated Kevin Nash (Vince Russo as guest referee)
- **Notes:** Russo tried to award Jarrett the title, but Nash stole it, leading to a match. Russo was the guest referee and counted the pin after Jarrett won. This was Jarrett's third WCW World Heavyweight Championship.
- **Source:** https://wrestlingrecaps.com/2011/01/02/wcw-nitro-5222000/

**May 23, 2000 - Jeff Jarrett to Kevin Nash (aired May 24)**
- **Event:** WCW Thunder (taped May 23) / aired May 24, 2000 (Wendler Arena, Saginaw, MI)
- **Match Type:** Triple Threat Match (Kevin Nash vs. Jeff Jarrett (c) vs. Scott Steiner)
- **Result:** Kevin Nash defeated Jeff Jarrett & Scott Steiner
- **Notes:** This was Nash's fourth WCW World Heavyweight Championship reign.
- **Source:** https://wrestlepedia.fandom.com/wiki/WCW_Thunder_2000

**May 29, 2000 - Kevin Nash to Ric Flair (awarded)**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Delta Center, Salt Lake City, UT)
- **Result:** Kevin Nash gave the title to Ric Flair
- **Notes:** Nash stated that Flair never really lost the title because Russo had stripped it from him. Nash put the belt on Flair's shoulder, declaring him champion. This was technically Flair's 16th World Championship reign (not recognized by WWE, who consider it one continuous reign).
- **Source:** https://www.thewrestlinginsomniac.com/2017/05/the-year-wcw-championship-changed-hands.html

**May 29, 2000 - Ric Flair to Jeff Jarrett**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Salt Lake City, UT)
- **Match Type:** Singles Match (Special Guest Referee: David Flair)
- **Result:** Jeff Jarrett defeated Ric Flair (c)
- **Notes:** The match was an overbooked mess with Vince Russo involved. Jarrett hit Flair with a guitar, Russo put on a ref shirt and counted the pin. This was Flair's last WCW World Championship reign ever. Jarrett's fourth reign lasted 41 days.
- **Source:** https://www.thesportster.com/wcw-nitro-three-world-champions-kevin-nash-ric-flair-jeff-jarrett/

## Major PPV Events

### WCW Spring Stampede 2000 (April 16, 2000)

**Location:** United Center, Chicago, Illinois
**Attendance:** 12,556 (8,377 paid - one-third of audience got in free)
**PPV Buyrate:** 115,000 (0.25) - highest buyrate in months, and the highest they would get for the rest of their existence

**Results:**
1. Ric Flair & The Total Package (w/ Elizabeth) defeated The Harris Brothers & The Mamalukes in Tag Team Tournament Semifinal (6:11)
2. Mancow defeated Jimmy Hart (2:48)
3. Scott Steiner defeated The Wall by DQ - US Title Tournament Quarterfinal (3:53)
4. Mike Awesome defeated Ernest "The Cat" Miller - US Title Tournament Quarterfinal (4:00)
5. Shane Douglas & Buff Bagwell defeated Harlem Heat 2000 (Stevie Ray & Big T) - Tag Team Tournament Semifinal (2:41)
6. Sting defeated Booker T - US Title Tournament Quarterfinal (6:34)
7. Vampiro defeated Billy Kidman - US Title Tournament Quarterfinal (8:28) [Hulk Hogan body-slammed Kidman through the announce table to cost him the match]
8. Terry Funk defeated Norman Smiley for vacant WCW Hardcore Championship (8:02)
9. Scott Steiner defeated Mike Awesome by submission - US Title Tournament Semifinal (3:14) [Kevin Nash hit Mike Awesome with a crutch to the back]
10. Sting defeated Vampiro by submission - US Title Tournament Semifinal (5:59)
11. Chris Candido (w/ Tammy Lynn Sytch) won Suicide Six-Way Match for vacant WCW Cruiserweight Championship (5:12)
12. Shane Douglas & Buff Bagwell (w/ Vince Russo) defeated Ric Flair & The Total Package for vacant WCW World Tag Team Championship (8:29) [KroniK debuted, double chokeslamming Luger]
13. Scott Steiner defeated Sting by technical submission for vacant WCW United States Heavyweight Championship (5:33) [Vampiro pulled Sting under the ring, bloodying him]
14. Jeff Jarrett defeated Diamond Dallas Page (w/ Kimberly) for vacant WCW World Heavyweight Championship (15:02) [Kimberly turned on DDP with a guitar shot]

**Key Notes:**
- This was the first PPV after the April 10 reboot
- All titles were vacant and filled via tournaments
- The New Blood dominated, winning all titles except Hardcore
- Hulk Hogan's interference in Kidman's match was a major angle
- KroniK debuted as enforcers for Vince Russo and the New Blood
- Tony Schiavone said "BULLSHIT" on air (reportedly muted)
- Eric Bischoff was shown backstage worried about Hogan checking out of the hospital
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Stampede_(2000)

### WCW Slamboree 2000 (May 7, 2000)

**Location:** Kemper Arena, Kansas City, Missouri
**Attendance:** 7,165
**PPV Buyrate:** 0.14
**Note:** This was the eighth and final Slamboree event

**Results:**
1. Chris Candido (c) (w/ Tammy Lynn Sytch) defeated The Artist (w/ Paisley) - WCW Cruiserweight Championship (7:59)
2. Terry Funk (c) defeated Norman Smiley & Ralphus - Handicap Hardcore Match - WCW Hardcore Championship (10:03)
3. Shawn Stasiak defeated Curt Hennig (7:54)
4. Scott Steiner (c) (w/ Midajah & Shakira) defeated General Rection - WCW United States Championship (9:24)
5. Mike Awesome vs. Chris Kanyon - No Contest (12:11) [Kevin Nash interfered, fought Awesome; other New Blood and Millionaire's Club members brawled]
6. The Total Package (w/ Elizabeth) defeated Buff Bagwell (9:30) [Miss Elizabeth interfered with a baseball bat; Chuck Palumbo attacked Package after the match]
7. Shane Douglas defeated Ric Flair (8:46) [David Flair, disguised as Sting, hit Ric with a baseball bat; Russo screamed "Start the clock!"]
8. Sting defeated Vampiro (6:49) [Sting returned to the ring after the match to attack Vampiro with a metal pipe]
9. Hulk Hogan defeated Billy Kidman (w/ Eric Bischoff as Special Guest Referee) (13:31) [Horace Hogan interfered; Bischoff was ejected earlier by Russo]
10. Jeff Jarrett defeated David Arquette (c) & Diamond Dallas Page - Triple Cage Match - WCW World Heavyweight Championship (15:29) [Arquette turned on Page with a guitar shot]

**Key Notes:**
- The main event featured the same Triple Cage match from *Ready to Rumble*
- David Arquette's title loss came after he turned on DDP
- David Flair turned on Ric Flair, disguised as Sting
- Shawn Stasiak's win over Curt Hennig was a breakout moment
- Miss Elizabeth attacked Vince Russo with a baseball bat
- The event was the final Slamboree in WCW history
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slamboree_(2000)

### WCW The Great American Bash 2000 (June 11, 2000)

**Location:** Baltimore Arena, Baltimore, Maryland
**Attendance:** 7,031
**PPV Buyrate:** 85,000 (0.19)
**Note:** This was the final Great American Bash produced by WCW

**Results:**
1. Lt. Loco (c) (w/ Misfits In Action) defeated Disco Inferno (w/ Filthy Animals) - WCW Cruiserweight Championship (4:57)
2. KroniK (Brian Adams & Bryan Clark) defeated The Mamalukes (Big Vito & Johnny the Bull) - #1 Contenders Tag Team Match (9:20)
3. Mike Awesome defeated Diamond Dallas Page (w/ Chris Kanyon) - Ambulance Match (9:41)
4. GI Bro defeated Shawn Stasiak - Boot Camp Match (13:58)
5. Shane Douglas defeated The Wall - Tables Match (8:12) [Douglas put The Wall through three tables at the same time]
6. Scott Steiner (c) (w/ Midajah & Shakira) defeated Rick Steiner & Tank Abbott - Handicap Asylum Match (3:46) [Scott forced Abbott to submit]
7. Hollywood Hogan defeated Billy Kidman (w/ Horace Hogan as Special Guest Referee) (11:39) [Hogan used brass knuckles; if Hogan lost, he would have to retire]
8. Ric Flair defeated David Flair (w/ Vince Russo) by submission (10:16) [If Ric lost, he would have to retire]
9. Vampiro defeated Sting - Human Torch Match (7:23) [Vampiro set Sting on fire]
10. Jeff Jarrett (c) defeated Kevin Nash (w/ Ernest Miller as Special Guest Enforcer) - WCW World Heavyweight Championship (17:22) [Jarrett pinned Nash after a Spear from Goldberg; Goldberg then joined The New Blood]

**Key Notes:**
- Goldberg's turn to join The New Blood was a major swerve
- Vampiro setting Sting on fire was the event's biggest visual spot
- Ric Flair's retirement was on the line (he won)
- Hollywood Hogan's retirement was on the line (he won, earning a title shot at Bash at the Beach)
- The event was widely panned critically
- Konnan was guest bellringer, Rey Mysterio Jr. was guest timekeeper, Disco Inferno was guest beltkeeper, Juventud Guerrera was guest ring announcer for the main event
- The card had a Cagematch rating of 1.52
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_American_Bash_(2000)

## Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### David Arquette as World Champion

**Incident:** Actor David Arquette was crowned WCW World Heavyweight Champion on April 25/26, 2000

**Details:**
- The decision was made to promote the film *Ready to Rumble*
- Arquette was against the idea, knowing fans would hate a non-wrestler winning the title
- Vince Russo insisted it would be good for publicity
- Gene Okerlund later stated "once Arquette won the title, it might as well been thrown in the trash"
- The decision is widely cited as a pivotal moment in WCW's demise
- Arquette's reign lasted only 12 days (until Slamboree on May 7)
- WWE Magazine listed it as the top reason for the "failure" of Nitro
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Arquette

### WCW Championship "Hot Potato" - May 2000

**Incident:** The WCW World Heavyweight Championship changed hands 5 times between May 15 and May 29, 2000

**Details:**
- May 15: Ric Flair won from Jeff Jarrett
- May 22: Russo stripped Flair, Jarrett won vacant title from Nash
- May 24 (aired): Nash won from Jarrett and Steiner (triple threat)
- May 29: Nash gave title to Flair (not a match)
- May 29: Jarrett won from Flair (same night!)
- Three different world champions were crowned on one Nitro episode (May 29)
- This destroyed whatever credibility the title had left
- **Source:** https://www.thewrestlinginsomniac.com/2017/05/the-year-wcw-championship-changed-hands.html

### Vince Russo's Booking

**Incident:** Vince Russo's hotshot booking style reached its peak

**Details:**
- Constant title changes, shock swerves, and convoluted storylines
- The New Blood vs. Millionaire's Club angle backfired as fans cheered the veterans
- WCW was averaging approximately 2.5-3.0 rating on Nitro while WWF's Raw drew more than double
- The booking prioritized short-term shock value over long-term coherence
- **Source:** https://grokipedia.com/page/New_Blood_(professional_wrestling)

### Ric Flair's Legitimate Health Issues

**Incident:** Ric Flair suffered a real inner ear equilibrium disorder

**Details:**
- On Thunder (May 23, aired May 24), Flair fell down after a match, showing visible balance problems
- WCW worked it into an angle, with Russo claiming Flair had a brain aneurysm
- Russo held a mock funeral for Flair's career
- Flair later required surgery for the inner ear issue
- The angle was widely criticized for exploiting a legitimate medical condition
- **Source:** https://www.thewrestlinginsomniac.com/2017/05/the-year-wcw-championship-changed-hands.html

### Goldberg's Turn to The New Blood

**Incident:** Bill Goldberg joined The New Blood at The Great American Bash (June 11)

**Details:**
- After spearing Kevin Nash to help Jeff Jarrett retain the World Title, Goldberg aligned with the New Blood
- This was one of the biggest swerves in WCW's final year
- Goldberg had been positioned as a babyface, making his turn shocking
- **Source:** https://forextreme.fandom.com/wiki/The_Great_American_Bash

### Mike Awesome Endangers Chris Kanyon

**Incident:** At Slamboree, Mike Awesome threw Kanyon off the second tier of the Triple Cage

**Details:**
- Kanyon had attempted to save DDP from a Jarrett/Awesome beatdown
- Awesome grabbed Kanyon and tossed him off the second cage level onto the entrance ramp
- This put Kanyon's life at great risk
- **Source:** https://princewatercresswrestling.blogspot.com/2000/05/slamboree-2000-results.html

### WCW's Financial Decline

**Incident:** WCW was hemorrhaging money throughout this period

**Details:**
- Inflated talent contracts consumed over 60% of revenues
- PPV buyrates were in freefall: Spring Stampede (115,000), Slamboree (0.14), Great American Bash (85,000)
- Nitro ratings averaged approximately 2.5-3.0 while WWF Raw drew more than double
- WWF had won the Monday Night Wars for 76 consecutive weeks
- Attendance was poor: Spring Stampede drew 12,556 but only 8,377 paid (one-third free)
- WCW would eventually be sold to WWF in March 2001
- **Source:** https://grokipedia.com/page/The_Great_American_Bash_(2000)

## Conflicts with Previous Reports

### January-March 2000 Report Updates
- **April 10, 2000:** The Jan-Mar report noted Sid Vicious held the World Title until April 10. This is confirmed - Russo & Bischoff stripped all titles on April 10, with Sid initially refusing before Bischoff threatened to fire him.
- **The Reboot:** The Jan-Mar report mentioned Vince Russo's hotshot booking continuing. The April 10 reboot was the culmination - Russo AND Bischoff returned together, going beyond hotshot booking to a complete restart.
- **Bret Hart:** The Jan-Mar report covered Hart's concussion. He did not appear during April-June. His final WCW appearance was September 6, 2000 (Thunder), and he officially retired in October 2000.
- **Jeff Jarrett:** Jan-Mar report noted Jarrett held the US Title. He lost it in the April 10 reboot, then won the World Title at Spring Stampede on April 16.
- **Diamond Dallas Page:** Jan-Mar report did not cover DDP in detail. He won the World Title from Jarrett on April 24, then lost it to Arquette on April 25/26.

## Conclusion

The April-June 2000 period represents the absolute nadir of WCW's creative and business operations. The New Blood vs. Millionaire's Club angle, while conceptually interesting (younger talent vs. overpaid veterans), backfired spectacularly as fans sided with the veterans. The David Arquette championship reign, while lasting only 12 days, became a symbol of everything wrong with WCW and was cited as a key factor in the company's demise. The World Heavyweight Championship changed hands 8 times in three months, including an unprecedented night where three different men held the title. WCW's ratings had collapsed to approximately 2.5-3.0 on Nitro while WWF's Raw drew more than double. PPV buyrates were in freefall (Spring Stampede: 115,000, Slamboree: 0.14, Great American Bash: 85,000). The period ended with Jeff Jarrett as World Champion heading into Bash at the Beach in July, where the infamous Hogan/Russo incident would occur. WCW would be sold to WWF in March 2001.
