## 1. Title Changes

### ECW World Heavyweight Championship — Taz → Mike Awesome

**Title Change: Taz → Mike Awesome**

- **Date:** September 19, 1999
- **Taping date:** September 19, 1999
- **Air date:** Shown live on PPV
- **Championship:** ECW World Heavyweight Championship
- **Winner:** Mike Awesome (w/ Judge Jeff Jones)
- **Loser:** Taz (c) and Masato Tanaka
- **Show:** ECW Anarchy Rulz 1999
- **Venue:** Odeum Expo Center
- **City:** Villa Park, Illinois
- **Attendance:** 6,000
- **Gate:** $208,000
- **Finish:** Mike Awesome defeated Masato Tanaka and Taz (c) in a Three-Way Dance to win the ECW World Heavyweight Championship (13:48). Originally a one-on-one match between Taz and Tanaka, but Taz spotted Mike Awesome in the crowd and invited him to join as the third competitor.
  - **Elimination 1:** Masato Tanaka and Mike Awesome eliminated Taz (c) — Roaring Elbow by Tanaka and Awesome Splash by Awesome, both covered Taz (2:01)
  - **Elimination 2:** Mike Awesome eliminated Masato Tanaka — Awesome Bomb through a table (13:48)
- **Event type:** Pay-per-view
- **Why it mattered:** This marked the end of Taz's 252-day ECW World Heavyweight Championship reign (beginning January 10, 1999 at Guilty as Charged). Taz had signed a WWF contract in September 1999 and dropped the title in his final ECW PPV match. After the match, Taz handed the belt to Awesome, shook hands, and embraced Paul Heyman. The crowd chanted for Taz as he left the right way. Mike Awesome, who was not even advertised for the show, shockingly won the title.

- **Why it mattered:** Heat Wave 1999 was ECW's third PPV of the year. The tag team title change and the RVD/Lynn vs. Impact Players main event were highlights. The Dudley Boyz post-match attack through flaming tables was a memorable visual moment on PPV.

### ECW The Last Show at the Madhouse — August 26, 1999

- **Date:** August 26, 1999
- **Venue:** Elks Lodge 878
- **City:** Queens, New York
- **Attendance:** ~1,000
- **Commentators:** Joey Styles and Joel Gertner
- **Main matches:**
  - ECW World Heavyweight Championship: Taz (c) defeated Yoshihiro Tajiri (w/ Jack Victory & Steve Corino) via submission (Tazmission) (4:18)
  - Danny Doring defeated CW Anderson
  - Uganda defeated Vito Lograsso
  - Super Crazy defeated Rhino (4:56)
  - Three-Way Dance: Chris Chetti and Nova defeated The FBI (Little Guido & Sal E. Graziano) and Simon Diamond & Tony DeVito
  - ECW World Tag Team Championship: The Dudley Boyz (Buh Buh Ray & D-Von) (w/ Sign Guy Dudley) defeated Balls Mahoney & Spike Dudley (c) to win the titles (5:22)
  - ECW World Television Championship: Rob Van Dam (w/ Bill Alfonso) (c) vs. Jerry Lynn ended in a no contest when The Impact Players interfered (11:01)
  - ECW World Tag Team Championship: Raven & Tommy Dreamer (w/ Francine) defeated The Dudley Boyz (Buh Buh Ray & D-Von) (c) (w/ Sign Guy Dudley) to win the titles (impromptu match)
- **Major finishes:** 
  - The Dudley Boyz won their eighth ECW Tag Team Championship in their final ECW appearance. After the match, they came to the ring, derided ECW, and threatened to hand the titles to WWF chairman Vince McMahon. They called out Tommy Dreamer and insulted him.
  - This resulted in an impromptu 2-on-1 handicap match. As the Dudley Boyz attempted a 3D on Dreamer, he reversed it into a DDT onto D-Von. Raven — absent from ECW since June 1997 — made a surprise return and DDT'd Buh Buh Ray. Dreamer and Raven simultaneously pinned both Dudley Boyz to become the new ECW World Tag Team Champions.
  - After the bout, a confused Dreamer was handed one of the title belts by Raven. Raven left through the crowd as the show ended.
- **Important debuts/returns/exits:** 
  - **Raven's return to ECW** — Raven made a shocking return after a two-year absence (last seen in June 1997 when he lost a "loser leaves town" match to Dreamer in WCW)
  - **The Dudley Boyz's final ECW appearance** — They departed for the WWF
- **Why it mattered:** The Last Show at the Madhouse was one of the most memorable events in ECW history. Raven's return on ECW's debut on TNN was a massive surprise, and the unlikely Dreamer/Raven tag team partnership began. The Dudley Boyz's departure for WWF marked a significant talent loss for ECW as it went national. This event was televised as the second episode of ECW on TNN on September 3, 1999.

### ECW on TNN Premiere — August 27 / September 3, 1999

- **Date:** First episode aired August 27, 1999; second episode (first with new content) aired September 3, 1999
- **Network:** TNN (The Nashville Network)
- **Timeslot:** Friday nights at 8 PM ET
- **Format:** Weekly one-hour program
- **Main matches (Episode 2, September 3, 1999 — the first with new match content):**
  - ECW World Heavyweight Championship: Taz (c) defeated Yoshihiro Tajiri to retain the title
  - ECW World Tag Team Championship: The Dudley Boyz defeated Spike Dudley & Balls Mahoney (c) to win the titles
  - ECW World Tag Team Championship: Raven & Tommy Dreamer defeated The Dudley Boyz (c) to win the titles (impromptu match — Raven's return)
- **Why it mattered:** ECW on TNN marked ECW's first national television deal — a three-year contract signed in July 1999. The deal was one-sided and restrictive, giving CBS (TNN's parent) a percentage of ECW's earnings while providing little financial support. ECW funded its own production. Despite the challenging terms, it represented a major milestone for ECW as it went national. The second episode (September 3) featured some of the most dramatic content in ECW history: the Dudley Boyz's departure, Raven's return, and a double tag team title change.
