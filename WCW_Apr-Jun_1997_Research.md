# WCW Research Report: April 1997 – June 1997

> **Note re: continuity with previous reports:** At the end of March 1997, the titleholders were:
> - WCW World Heavyweight: **"Hollywood" Hogan** (nWo) (held since Hog Wild, 8/10/1996)
> - WCW US Heavyweight: **Dean Malenko** (won from Eddie Guerrero at Uncensored, 3/16/1997)
> - WCW World Tag Team: **The Outsiders** (Hall & Nash) (nWo)
> - WCW World Television: **Prince Iaukea** (won from Regal on Nitro, 2/17/1997)
> - WCW Cruiserweight: **Syxx** (nWo) (won from Malenko at SuperBrawl VII, 2/23/1997)
> - WCW Women's: **Akira Hokuto** (inaugural, 12/29/1996)
>
> **[CONFLICT WITH PREVIOUS REPORT]:** The Jan–Mar 1997 report stated Iaukea "held through this period" but the actual TV title lineage shows Lord Steven Regal was the incoming champion for 1997 (per Wikipedia). Regal retained on Feb 10 vs. Iaukea, then Iaukea won it back on Feb 17. This means the title changed from Iaukea → Regal at some point between late 1996 and Feb 10, 1997 — a change not documented in any previous report. The exact date is uncertain.

## 1. Title Changes

### 1A. WCW World Television Championship — Prince Iaukea → Último Dragón

- **Date:** April 7, 1997
- **Championship:** WCW World Television Championship
- **Winner:** Último Dragón (w/ Sonny Onoo)
- **Loser:** Prince Iaukea (c)
- **Show:** WCW Monday Nitro #82
- **Air date:** April 7, 1997 (LIVE)
- **Venue:** Johnson City, Tennessee
- **Details:** Último Dragón pinned Iaukea to win the TV Title. Dragón was already the J-Crown champion (holder of 8 junior heavyweight titles). This was part of WCW's push to bring more international junior heavyweights to US television.
- **Source:**
  - https://wrestlepedia.fandom.com/wiki/WCW_Nitro_1997

### 1B. WCW World Television Championship — Último Dragón → Lord Steven Regal

- **Championship:** WCW World Television Championship

## 3. Roster Moves / Debuts / Exits

### 3A. Dennis Rodman — nWo Association (Ongoing April–June 1997)

- **Date:** Ongoing April–June 1997
- **Person:** Dennis Rodman (NBA player, Chicago Bulls)
- **Change:** Heavily associated with the nWo during this period. Appeared at events, was in the movie "Double Team" (with nWo members), and teamed with Hogan on Nitro (June 16).
- **Impact:** Major celebrity crossover. Rodman's association with the nWo brought mainstream attention. But it also showed how far the nWo had permeated pop culture.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://grokipedia.com/page/Spring_Stampede_(1997) ("a preview clip of 'Double Team,' starring Jean Claude Van Damme and the nWo's Dennis Rodman would air the following night on Monday Nitro")
  - https://thehistoryofwwe.com/wcw-results-1997/

### 3B. Reggie White — One-Off Appearance (May 18, 1997)

- **Date:** May 18, 1997 (Slamboree)
- **Person:** Reggie White (NFL legend, Green Bay Packers)
- **Change:** Lost to Steve McMichael at Slamboree (15:17) in an NFL-vs-WCW crossover match. Gilbert Brown (another NFL player) interfered on McMichael's behalf.
- **Impact:** NFL crossover was a major mainstream moment. McMichael (former NFL player) vs. White (NFL legend) was the angle.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slamboree_(1997)
  - https://www.pwwew.net/ppv/wcw/may/1997.htm

### 3C. Kevin Greene — WCW In-Ring (Ongoing)

- **Date:** Ongoing April–June 1997
- **Person:** Kevin Greene (NFL player, Carolina Panthers)
- **Change:** Continued his WCW involvement, beating Steve McMichael at Great American Bash (June 15). Was part of Flair/Piper/Greene team at Slamboree.
- **Impact:** NFL crossover continued. Greene was being positioned as a WCW loyalist against the nWo.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slamboree_(1997)
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_American_Bash_(1997)

### 3D. Sonny Onoo Turns on Último Dragón (May 18, 1997)

- **Date:** May 18, 1997 (Slamboree)
- **Person:** Sonny Onoo (manager)
- **Change:** Turned on his client Último Dragón during the TV Title match, costing Dragón the title and allowing Lord Steven Regal to win.
- **Impact:** Dragón lost the TV Title. Onoo's betrayal was a shock. This began the end of Dragón's dominant WCW run — he would leave WCW later in 1997.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slamboree_(1997)
  - https://www.pwwew.net/ppv/wcw/may/1997.htm ("After Onoo turned on The Ultimo Dragon, Regal Suplexed him and did the Regal Stretch")

## 4. Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### 4A. Scott Steiner — Arrested (April 1997)

- **Date:** April 1997 (announced at Spring Stampede)
- **Person:** Scott Steiner
- **What happened:** Scott Steiner was arrested. At Spring Stampede, it was announced that Rick Steiner would face Kevin Nash alone for half the tag titles. Steiner's absence lasted approximately 5 months.
- **Why it mattered:** The Steiner Brothers were one of WCW's top tag teams. Scott's absence weakened the babyface tag division.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://grokipedia.com/page/Spring_Stampede_(1997) ("it was announced Scott Steiner had been arrested and Rick Steiner would face Kevin Nash later in the show for the WCW Tag Team Titles")
  - https://thehistoryofwwe.com/wcw-spring-stampede-results/

### 4B. nWo Dominance — No Major Title Losses in This Period

- **Date:** April–June 1997 (ongoing)
- **What happened:** The nWo retained ALL their titles throughout this period. Every champion retained at Spring Stampede. Outsiders retained tag titles at Great American Bash. Only the TV Title changed hands (from Iaukea to Dragón to Regal — not nWo related). The US Title changed from Malenko to Jarrett (not nWo related).
- **Why it mattered:** The nWo's dominance was absolute. Babyfaces couldn't win titles. This was creating viewer fatigue.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://grokipedia.com/page/Spring_Stampede_(1997) ("No championship changes occurred during the event")
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_American_Bash_(1997) (no title changes)

### 4C. Spring Stampede — Zero Title Changes

- **Date:** April 6, 1997
- **What happened:** Every single champion retained at Spring Stampede. No title changes at all.
- **Why it mattered:** This was unusual and frustrating for fans. A PPV with zero title changes felt stagnant.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://grokipedia.com/page/Spring_Stampede_(1997) ("No championship changes occurred during the event")
