## Executive Summary

The July-September 2004 period was defined by Randy Orton's historic rise to become the youngest World Heavyweight Champion in WWE history at age 24, JBL's controversial win of the WWE Championship to begin his 280-day reign, and the dramatic breakup of Evolution after Orton was kicked out of the stable. This quarter featured three major PPV events: **The Great American Bash** (June 27, Norfolk, Virginia), **SummerSlam** (August 15, Toronto, Ontario, Canada), and **Unforgiven** (September 12, Portland, Oregon).

**Key developments included:**
- **JBL** won the WWE Championship from Eddie Guerrero in a controversial Texas Bullrope match at The Great American Bash, beginning his 280-day reign
- **Randy Orton** defeated Chris Benoit at SummerSlam to become the youngest World Heavyweight Champion in WWE history at age 24
- **Evolution** kicked out Randy Orton the night after SummerSlam (August 16) in the infamous "thumbs up, thumbs down" moment
- **Triple H** defeated Randy Orton at Unforgiven to win his 9th World Heavyweight Championship
- **Chris Jericho** won the vacant Intercontinental Championship in a ladder match at Unforgiven
- **Rey Mysterio** retained the Cruiserweight Championship in a highly praised match vs. Chavo Guerrero

**PPV Statistics:**
- **The Great American Bash:** 6,500 attendance (5,500 paid), 238,000 buys
- **SummerSlam:** 415,000 buys
- **Unforgiven:** 239,000 buys

## Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### JBL's Controversial WWE Championship Win
At The Great American Bash, JBL won the WWE Championship from Eddie Guerrero in a controversial Texas Bullrope match. Guerrero was initially declared the winner, but General Manager Kurt Angle came out and showed on the JumboTron that JBL had touched the final turnbuckle with his shoulder before Guerrero. This controversial finish was criticized as a "travesty" due to the abrupt heel champion push amid fan backlash. The match received ***¼ stars from Meltzer but was labeled controversial.

### Randy Orton Becomes Youngest World Champion
At SummerSlam, Randy Orton defeated Chris Benoit to become the youngest World Heavyweight Champion in WWE history at age 24. This was a historic moment that sent shockwaves through the wrestling world. After the match, Benoit offered Orton a handshake and told him to "be a man," which Orton accepted. This moment was seen as Benoit passing the torch to the next generation.

### Evolution Kicks Out Randy Orton
On August 16, 2004 (the night after SummerSlam), Evolution kicked out Randy Orton in the infamous "thumbs up, thumbs down" moment. During a mock celebration of Orton's title win, Triple H gave him a thumbs up but abruptly changed it to a thumbs down. Evolution then attacked Orton, kicking him out of the stable. This was due to Triple H's jealousy of Orton's success and refusal to let someone else hold the championship that was supposed to belong to him.

### Kurt Angle's El Gran Luchadore Gimmick
After JBL's controversial title win, Eddie Guerrero began a feud with Kurt Angle. During a Steel Cage match between Guerrero and JBL, El Gran Luchadore (Kurt Angle in disguise) interfered on JBL's behalf, allowing JBL to escape the cage and win the match. After the match, Guerrero attacked El Gran Luchadore and pulled off his mask, revealing him to be Kurt Angle.

### Orton's Historic but Brief Reign
Randy Orton's World Heavyweight Championship reign lasted only 28 days, the shortest in the title's history at that point. He won the title at SummerSlam (August 15) and lost it to Triple H at Unforgiven (September 12). Despite the brief reign, it was historically significant as Orton became the youngest world champion in WWE history.

### Triple H's 9th World Title
At Unforgiven, Triple H defeated Randy Orton to win his 9th World Heavyweight Championship. This further cemented Triple H's status as one of the most decorated champions in WWE history. The victory came after Triple H had kicked Orton out of Evolution and demanded he surrender the championship.

## Conclusion

The July-September 2004 period was defined by Randy Orton's historic rise to become the youngest World Heavyweight Champion in WWE history, JBL's controversial win of the WWE Championship to begin his 280-day reign, and the dramatic breakup of Evolution after Orton was kicked out of the stable. The quarter opened with The Great American Bash, where JBL won the WWE Championship from Eddie Guerrero in a controversial Texas Bullrope match with interference from Kurt Angle.

SummerSlam was highlighted by Randy Orton's victory over Chris Benoit to become the youngest World Heavyweight Champion at age 24. The post-match moment where Benoit offered Orton a handshake and told him to "be a man" was seen as a passing of the torch to the next generation. However, the celebration was short-lived as Evolution kicked out Orton the very next night on Raw in the infamous "thumbs up, thumbs down" moment.

Unforgiven featured Triple H defeating Randy Orton to win his 9th World Heavyweight Championship, ending Orton's historic but brief 28-day reign. Chris Jericho won the vacant Intercontinental Championship in a ladder match, and various other feuds continued.

The period also saw the beginning of JBL's 280-day WWE Championship reign, which was positioned as a controversial heel championship emphasizing his "Wrestling God" persona. Kurt Angle's El Gran Luchadore gimmick and interference in JBL's matches added to the controversy surrounding the title change.

By the end of September 2004, the championship landscape was: JBL (WWE Champion, SmackDown!), Triple H (World Heavyweight Champion, Raw), Chris Jericho (Intercontinental Champion, Raw), La Résistance (World Tag Team Champions, Raw), [WWE Tag Team Champions - needs verification], Trish Stratus (Women's Champion, Raw), Rey Mysterio (Cruiserweight Champion, SmackDown!), and John Cena (United States Champion, SmackDown!).

The quarter marked a significant transition period in WWE, with the end of Chris Benoit's historic World Heavyweight Championship reign, the beginning of JBL's long WWE Championship reign, and Randy Orton's emergence as a top star after being kicked out of Evolution.
