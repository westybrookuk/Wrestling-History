# WCW Research Report: July 1997 – September 1997

> **Note re: continuity with previous reports:** At the end of June 1997, the titleholders were:
> - WCW World Heavyweight: **"Hollywood" Hogan** (nWo) (held since Hog Wild, 8/10/1996)
> - WCW US Heavyweight: **Jeff Jarrett** (won from Malenko on Nitro, 6/9/1997)
> - WCW World Tag Team: **The Outsiders** (Hall & Nash) (nWo) (held since Halloween Havoc, 10/27/1996)
> - WCW World Television: **Lord Steven Regal** (won from Dragón at Slamboree, 5/18/1997)
> - WCW Cruiserweight: **Chris Jericho** (won from Dragón, exact date uncertain — between late June and mid-July 1997)
> - WCW Women's: **Akira Hokuto** (inaugural, 12/29/1996)
>
> **[NOTE FROM PREVIOUS REPORT]:** The TV title lineage shows Regal won from Dragón at Slamboree (May 18). Dragón had previously won from Iaukea on April 7. The Jericho Cruiserweight reign began between late June and mid-July.

### 1E. WCW World Heavyweight Championship — Hollywood Hogan → Lex Luger → Hollywood Hogan

- **Date (Luger wins):** August 4, 1997
- **Date (Hogan wins back):** August 9, 1997
- **Championship:** WCW World Heavyweight Championship
- **Winner (Aug 4):** Lex Luger
- **Loser (Aug 4):** "Hollywood" Hogan (c) (nWo)
- **Winner (Aug 9):** "Hollywood" Hogan
- **Loser (Aug 9):** Lex Luger (c)
- **Shows:**
  - Aug 4: WCW Monday Nitro #100 (100th episode!) — Auburn Hills, Michigan
  - Aug 9: WCW Road Wild 1997 (PPV) — Sturgis, South Dakota
- **Details (Aug 4):** Lex Luger submitted Hogan with the Torture Rack at approximately 13:00 to win the World Title. This was the FIRST TIME Hogan had lost the title on live television in over a year. The entire WCW locker room celebrated. The nWo spray paint was washed off the Big Gold Belt. Luger was doused with champagne. Hogan's reign lasted 359 days (Aug 10, 1996 – Aug 4, 1997).
- **Details (Aug 9):** Hogan defeated Luger at 16:15 (some sources say 16:14) to win the title back. A fake Sting appeared and hit Luger with a baseball bat, allowing Hogan to capitalize. Luger's reign lasted only **5-6 days** — one of the shortest World Title reigns in wrestling history.
- **Why it mattered:**
  - Luger's win on Nitro #100 was one of the biggest moments in WCW history — the first time Hogan had lost the title on live TV in over a year.
  - The celebration was electric. The entire locker room came out.
  - But Hogan won it back 5 days later at Road Wild, making Luger's reign feel meaningless.
  - Per Luger himself: "I didn't learn until I basically went to the ring that I was going to win the World Title that night."
  - Per Hogan (via multiple sources): He agreed to drop the title on two conditions: (1) he'd get it back ASAP, and (2) he'd choose who he dropped it to. He chose Luger because he "hated him the least."

  - Goldberg's streak would eventually reach 173-0 (officially) before ending at Starrcade 1998.

## 4. Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### 4A. Luger's 5-Day World Title Reign

- **Date:** August 4–9, 1997
- **What happened:** Luger won the World Title on Nitro #100 (Aug 4), lost it back to Hogan at Road Wild (Aug 9). Reign lasted only 5-6 days.
- **Why it mattered:**
  - The win on Nitro #100 was legendary — the celebration, the locker room, the washing of the nWo paint.
  - But losing it back 5 days later made the whole thing feel meaningless.
  - Per multiple sources, Hogan only agreed to drop the title on the condition he'd get it back immediately and could choose who he dropped it to.
  - This wasted Luger's biggest career moment.
  - Many fans feel this finish should have been saved for Starrcade instead.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://www.thesportster.com/wcw-why-lex-luger-short-world-championship-reign-was-perfect/
  - https://www.reddit.com/r/WCW/comments/1jckm0p/lex_luger_winning_the_wcw_world_title_from/

### 4B. Goldberg's Streak — Numbers Inflated

- **Date:** Ongoing from June 1997
- **What happened:** WCW began inflating Goldberg's win streak numbers. The official count was 173-0, but the actual count was closer to 155-156. WCW included dark matches, house shows, and even fabricated wins.
- **Why it mattered:**
  - Fans caught on and began making their own counts.
  - The inflation damaged the streak's credibility.
  - Chris Jericho: "One week he'd be 42–0 and seven days later he'd be 58–0. Did stepping on bugs count?"
  - Despite the inflation, the streak was still a genuine phenomenon.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
