## Executive Summary

The October-December 2005 period was the darkest quarter in WWE history. On **November 13, 2005**, **Eddie Guerrero was found dead in his hotel room at the Marriott Hotel City Center in Minneapolis, Minnesota**, aged 38. His nephew Chavo Guerrero Jr. — who wrestled under the masked persona **Kerwin White** — found him unconscious; an autopsy later attributed the death to acute heart failure caused by arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease. The following night WWE aired tribute editions of Raw and SmackDown!, with storylines suspended.

Eddie Guerrero's death dominated the remaining six weeks of the quarter. He had won the WWE Championship at No Way Out 2004, wrestled the main events of SummerSlam 2005 just ten weeks earlier, and was the scheduled special guest referee for the World Heavyweight Championship at Survivor Series six weeks after his death. Batista, who had defeated him at No Mercy (October 9), was originally booked to defend the title against Eddie Guerrero and Randy Orton in a triple threat; the match never took place.

**Key developments included:**
- **Eddie Guerrero died on November 13, 2005** in Minneapolis, aged 38 — the most significant real-world event in WWE's 2005 calendar
- **WWE suspended all storylines** and aired tribute editions of Raw (November 14, 2005) and SmackDown! (November 18, 2005), both taped on November 13
- **No Mercy 2005 (October 9)** in Houston saw Batista retain the World Heavyweight Championship against Eddie Guerrero (18:40) — the last time the two wrestled each other
- **Survivor Series 2005 (November 27)** in Detroit drew 15,000 and approximately 400,000 buys, the fourth-highest buyrate of 2005
- **Kurt Angle defeated John Cena by pinfall** at Survivor Series (13:56) to retain the WWE Championship, with Daivari serving as special referee
- **Team SmackDown! defeated Team Raw** 5-on-5 (24:01), with Randy Orton the sole survivor — the pivotal match of the quarter
- **Booker T defeated Chris Benoit** in Match 1 of the Best-of-Seven series for the vacant United States Championship (14:39)
- **Trish Stratus defeated Melina** to retain the Women's Championship (6:30)
- **Benoit defeated Triple H** in a Guerrero tribute match on the November 18 SmackDown!, after which Benoit, Triple H and Dean Malenko pointed to the sky
- **Armageddon 2005 (December 18)** in Providence saw **The Undertaker defeat Randy Orton in a 31:31 Hell in a Cell match**
- **Bobby Lashley won the SmackDown! Championship** during the quarter (date requires verification — see Uncertain Facts)
- **Juventud Guerrero lost the Cruiserweight Championship to Kid Kash** at Armageddon
- **Randy Orton won the World Tag Team Championship** with Bobby Lashley and Mark Jindrak (date requires verification)

**Eddie Guerrero's Death (November 13, 2005):**
On November 13, 2005, **Chavo Guerrero Jr.** found **Eddie Guerrero** unconscious in his hotel room at the **Marriott Hotel City Center in Minneapolis, Minnesota**. Chavo later explained that Guerrero had passed out in his hotel bathroom with a toothbrush in his hand and that the basin water was running; he added that his uncle was still alive when discovered. On the arrival of the ambulance he was pronounced dead at the age of **38** (born October 9, 1967). **An autopsy revealed his death was the result of acute heart failure**, attributed to arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease. He had been clean from drugs for four years, but long-term substance abuse had left scarring on his heart.

**Guerrero's final televised match** was a SmackDown! match taped on November 8, 2005 and aired November 11, 2005, in which he defeated Mr. Kennedy by disqualification. **Caveat:** Third-party sources variously identify the opponent as Mr. Kennedy or JBL — see Uncertain Facts.

**Tribute broadcasts:**
- **Raw — November 14, 2005** and **SmackDown! — November 18, 2005** were both **filmed on November 13** and each aired as a tribute to Guerrero
- **All storylines were put on hold** and WWE employees were not required to perform, although several matches took place, including one featuring Chavo
- On the SmackDown! tribute show, **Chris Benoit defeated Triple H** in a tribute match, after which Benoit, Triple H and Dean Malenko assembled in the ring and pointed to the sky in salute of Guerrero
- **TNA dedicated its Genesis event** (aired the evening of Guerrero's death) to him
- **Ring of Honor renamed its next show** "Night of Tribute"
- **Ohio Valley Wrestling**, WWE's developmental territory, also paid tribute on its television taping
- Wrestlers primarily Guerrero's nephew Chavo and friends Rey Mysterio and Christian Cage began paying tribute in matches by using his **Frog Splash** finisher

## Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### Eddie Guerrero's Death (November 13, 2005) — The Defining Event of the Quarter
**Details:**
- **Date:** November 13, 2005
- **Location:** Marriott Hotel City Center, Minneapolis, Minnesota
- **Age:** 38 (born October 9, 1967)
- **Discovered by:** Chavo Guerrero Jr. (wrestling as Kerwin White at the time)
- **Cause of death:** Acute heart failure, attributed by the autopsy to arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease
- **Circumstances:** Found unconscious in the hotel bathroom with a toothbrush in his hand and the basin water running; Chavo stated he was still alive when discovered. Pronounced dead on the arrival of the ambulance

**Impact:**
- **Raw (November 14, 2005)** and **SmackDown! (November 18, 2005)** were both **filmed on November 13** and aired as tribute shows
- **All storylines were put on hold**; WWE employees were not required to perform, though several matches took place including one featuring Chavo
- **TNA dedicated its Genesis event**, aired the evening of his death, to Guerrero
- **Ring of Honor renamed its next show** "Night of Tribute"
- **Ohio Valley Wrestling** paid tribute on its television taping
- Guerrero had been scheduled as **special guest referee** for the World Heavyweight Championship match at Survivor Series
- A planned World Heavyweight Championship triple threat (Batista vs. Eddie Guerrero vs. Randy Orton) on the November 18, 2005 episode of SmackDown! was cancelled outright
- **Postscript:** WWE elected to use Guerrero's death to propel **Rey Mysterio**, who won the 2006 Royal Rumble match entering at number two in a lowrider as a tribute to his friend

### The Cancelled Triple Threat Match
Batista was booked to defend the World Heavyweight Championship against **Eddie Guerrero and Randy Orton** on the November 18, 2005 episode of SmackDown!. The match never took place because of Guerrero's death on November 13, the date of the taping. This is a canonical booking that was **written and then abandoned** — an important data point for any simulation.

### Armageddon 2005's Weak Card
Armageddon 2005 (December 18) was held one month after Guerrero's death and was widely criticised. A **Santa Claus impersonator cut a promo**, and a portion of the live crowd was reportedly disengaged. Contemporary reviews rated the show poorly.

### Batista's Injury and Subsequent Vacancy (January 2006 — Outside This Period)
For lineage completeness: on **January 6, 2006**, Batista tore his right triceps in a match against Mark Henry. He was forced to **vacate the World Heavyweight Championship at the SmackDown! tapings on January 13, 2006**, two days after surpassing Triple H's 280-day record for the longest championship reign. **Kurt Angle**, appearing on Raw for a Battle Royal, was declared the new champion by entering as a surprise participant and winning the 20-man Battle Royal for the vacant title. Batista himself quit WWE in June 2006 after Triple H denied him a shot at the World Heavyweight Championship.

### The Best-of-Seven Device

## Conclusion

The October-December 2005 period was defined by a single event: **Eddie Guerrero's death on November 13, 2005 in Minneapolis, aged 38**. His body was found by his nephew Chavo Guerrero Jr. — who was then wrestling as Kerwin White — in a hotel bathroom at the Marriott Hotel City Center. The autopsy attributed the death to acute heart failure caused by arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease. WWE suspended all storylines and aired tribute editions of Raw (November 14) and SmackDown! (November 18), both filmed on the day of the death. TNA's Genesis and a Ring of Honor show were also dedicated to him.

The quarter opened with **No Mercy 2005** (October 9) in Houston, where **Batista defeated Eddie Guerrero** (18:40) to retain the World Heavyweight Championship — the last time the two wrestled each other. Juventud Guerrero won the Cruiserweight Championship there, and Randy and Bob Orton defeated The Undertaker in a Handicap Casket Match. **Taboo Tuesday** (October 25) featured Mick Foley's return as three separate personas in a fan-voted match against Carlito, and saw The Big Show and Kane win the World Tag Team Championship.

**Survivor Series 2005** (November 27) at the Joe Louis Arena in Detroit drew 15,000 fans and approximately 400,000 buys. Team SmackDown! defeated Team Raw 5-on-5 with **Randy Orton as the sole survivor**, John Cena retained the WWE Championship against Kurt Angle under a hostile referee (Daivari), Booker T won Match 1 of the Best-of-Seven series for the vacant United States Championship, Trish Stratus retained the Women's Championship, and Theodore Long defeated Eric Bischoff. **Armageddon 2005** (December 18) in Providence closed the year with **The Undertaker defeating Randy Orton in a 31:31 Hell in a Cell match** and **Kid Kash winning the Cruiserweight Championship**. The event's card was widely criticised.

By the end of December 2005, the championship landscape was: **John Cena** (WWE Champion, Raw), **Batista** (World Heavyweight Champion, SmackDown! — the longest reign in brand history at 289 days, heading for 294), **Ric Flair** (Intercontinental Champion, Raw), **Randy Orton, Bobby Lashley & Mark Jindrak** (World Tag Team Champions, Raw), **Batista & Rey Mysterio Jr.** (WWE Tag Team Champions, SmackDown!), **Trish Stratus** (Women's Champion, Raw), **Kid Kash** (Cruiserweight Champion, SmackDown!), **Bobby Lashley** (SmackDown! Champion), and the **United States Championship vacant** pending the conclusion of the Booker T / Chris Benoit Best-of-Seven series.
