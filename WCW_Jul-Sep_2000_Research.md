# WCW July-September 2000 Research Report

**Period Covered:** July 1, 2000 - September 30, 2000

## Executive Summary

The July-September 2000 period began with the most infamous event in WCW's final year: Bash at the Beach 2000 (July 9), where the Hogan/Russo incident occurred. Hulk Hogan used his creative control clause to force Jeff Jarrett to lay down, winning the WCW World Heavyweight Championship. Vince Russo then cut a worked-shoot promo declaring Hogan "fired" and stripping the title, leading to Booker T winning his first World Title. This incident caused Eric Bischoff to leave WCW, Hogan to file a defamation lawsuit, and marked the effective end of the New Blood vs. Millionaire's Club angle. The period saw the rise of Lance Storm and Team Canada, with Storm simultaneously holding the US Title, Hardcore Title, and Cruiserweight Title. Booker T's first reign as World Champion was a bright spot, but was undermined by Russo booking himself as champion on September 25. Goldberg told Russo to "f*** off" on live TV at New Blood Rising, and later speared Russo through a cage to cost Booker the title. The period included three PPVs (Bash at the Beach, New Blood Rising, Fall Brawl) and ended with Russo having vacated the title (October 2) and the company in terminal decline.

## Title Changes

### WCW World Heavyweight Championship

**July 9, 2000 - Jeff Jarrett to Hollywood Hogan (reversed)**
- **Event:** WCW Bash at the Beach 2000 (Ocean Center, Daytona Beach, FL)
- **Match Type:** Singles Match
- **Result:** Hollywood Hogan pinned Jeff Jarrett (1:19)
- **Notes:** The most infamous moment in WCW's final year. Hogan used his creative control clause to force Jarrett to lay down. Russo threw the belt into the ring and told Hogan to pin Jarrett. Hogan complied but was visibly confused. After Hogan left the ring, Russo cut a worked-shoot promo declaring Hogan "fired," calling him a "piece of shit" and saying fans would "never see that piece of shit again." Russo vacated the title, calling it the "Hulk Hogan Memorial Belt." This was Hogan's final WCW appearance. Hogan filed a defamation lawsuit (dismissed in 2003). Booker T later stated he believed Russo "went into business for himself" that night, and that Hogan did NOT know about the plan beforehand. Bischoff and Eric were reportedly NOT in on Russo's extended promo.
- **Attendance:** 6,572
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bash_at_the_Beach_(2000)

**July 9, 2000 - Vacated to Booker T**
- **Event:** WCW Bash at the Beach 2000 (Ocean Center, Daytona Beach, FL)
- **Match Type:** Singles Match (Booker T vs. Jeff Jarrett)
- **Result:** Booker T defeated Jeff Jarrett (c) (13:41)
- **Notes:** After Russo vacated the title, an impromptu match was made. Booker T defeated Jarrett to win his first WCW World Heavyweight Championship. Russo raised Booker's hand. Booker T later called this "my proudest moment" and said "it was worth the lawsuit." Russo had previously polled the booking committee on who should win the title, and it was unanimous for Booker T.
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bash_at_the_Beach_(2000)

**August 28, 2000 - Booker T to Kevin Nash**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Tingley Coliseum / Pan American Center, Las Cruces, NM)
- **Result:** Kevin Nash defeated Booker T to win the title
- **Notes:** This was Nash's fifth WCW World Heavyweight Championship reign. Booker T had held the title for 50 days.
- **Source:** https://www.thewrestlinginsomniac.com/2017/05/the-year-wcw-championship-changed-hands.html

**September 17, 2000 - Kevin Nash to Booker T**
- **Event:** WCW Fall Brawl 2000 (HSBC Arena, Buffalo, NY)
- **Match Type:** Caged Heat Match (Steel Cage)
- **Result:** Booker T defeated Kevin Nash (c) (9:02)
- **Notes:** Booker T pinned Nash after the Book End. This was Booker T's second WCW World Heavyweight Championship reign.
- **Attendance:** 8,638
- **PPV Buyrate:** 0.16
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_Brawl_(2000)

**September 25, 2000 - Booker T to Vince Russo**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Nassau Coliseum, Uniondale, NY)
- **Match Type:** Steel Cage Match
- **Result:** Vince Russo defeated Booker T
- **Notes:** Goldberg speared Russo through the cage wall. Since Russo "escaped" the cage (via the spear), he was declared the winner. Russo was never announced as champion during the broadcast. On Thunder (September 27), Russo appeared with the belt declaring himself champion and retired from in-ring competition. The title was vacated the following Monday (October 2). This was widely regarded as the lowest point in WCW history - a WRITER holding the World Title. Russo later said the angle was "so well thought out."
- **Source:** https://www.cagesideseats.com/2016/9/25/12971208/this-day-in-wrestling-history-sept-25-vince-russo-wins-the-wcw-world-title

## Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### The Hogan/Russo Incident (Bash at the Beach, July 9)

**Incident:** The most infamous moment in WCW's final year

**Details:**
- Hogan wanted to win the World Title and leave as champion, invoking his creative control clause
- Originally, Jarrett was supposed to beat Hogan, but Hogan refused at the 12th hour
- Russo told Jarrett to lay down, Hogan pinned him (1:19)
- After Hogan left the ring, Russo cut an unscripted (or semi-scripted) worked-shoot promo
- Russo called Hogan a "piece of shit," said fans would "never see that piece of shit again"
- Russo vacated the title, calling it the "Hulk Hogan Memorial Belt"
- Hogan filed a defamation lawsuit (dismissed 2003)
- Bischoff left WCW shortly after
- Booker T later stated he didn't believe Hogan knew about Russo's plan beforehand
- The truth of what happened remains disputed to this day
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bash_at_the_Beach_(2000)

### Goldberg Tells Russo to "F*** Off" (New Blood Rising, August 13)

**Incident:** Goldberg confronted Vince Russo on live PPV

**Details:**
- During the Triple Threat Match (Nash vs. Goldberg vs. Steiner), Goldberg left the ring
- Russo went to force him back, and Goldberg told Russo to "f*** off" on live television
- This was a real moment of frustration, reflecting backstage tensions
- Scott Hudson called Goldberg a "crybaby" on air
- **Source:** https://atomicdrop.wordpress.com/2016/09/27/wcw-new-blood-rising-august-13-2000/

## Conflicts with Previous Reports

### April-June 2000 Report Updates
- **Jeff Jarrett's World Title reign:** The Apr-Jun report noted Jarrett's 4th reign began May 29. This is confirmed - Jarrett held the title through Bash at the Beach (July 9), where he lost to Hogan (reversed) and then to Booker T.
- **Scott Steiner's US Title:** The Apr-Jun report noted Steiner held the US Title. This is confirmed through Bash at the Beach (July 9), where he was stripped after using the banned Steiner Recliner.
- **The New Blood vs. Millionaire's Club:** The angle effectively ended after Bash at the Beach with Hogan's departure and Bischoff's exit.
- **KroniK's Tag Title reign:** Confirmed - they won the titles at Bash at the Beach (July 9) from The Perfect Event.
- **Booker T:** The Apr-Jun report noted Booker T as G.I. Bro. Booker T won his first World Title on July 9, 2000.
- **Bret Hart:** The Apr-Jun report noted Hart's retirement. His final WCW appearance was at New Blood Rising (August 13) and Thunder (September 6).

## Conclusion

The July-September 2000 period was defined by the Hogan/Russo incident at Bash at the Beach, which effectively ended the New Blood vs. Millionaire's Club angle, caused Eric Bischoff to leave WCW, and led to Hulk Hogan's defamation lawsuit. Booker T's first World Title reign was a genuine bright spot, but was undermined by Russo's self-serving booking, culminating in Russo booking himself as World Champion on September 25. Lance Storm emerged as a surprising star, holding three titles simultaneously and forming Team Canada with Bret Hart's blessing. The period included three PPVs (Bash at the Beach, New Blood Rising, Fall Brawl) and saw WCW's ratings and buyrates continue to plummet. The period ended with Russo concussed by Goldberg's spear, the World Title vacated (October 2), and the company in terminal decline heading toward its March 2001 sale to WWF.
