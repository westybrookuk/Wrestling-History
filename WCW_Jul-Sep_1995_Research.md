# WCW Research Report: July 1995 – September 1995

> **Note re: continuity with previous reports:** At the end of June 1995, the titleholders were:
> - WCW World Heavyweight: **Hulk Hogan** (held since 7/17/1994)
> - WCW US Heavyweight: **Sting** (won vacant title at Great American Bash, 6/18/1995)
> - WCW World Tag Team: **Harlem Heat** (Booker T & Stevie Ray) — regained from Nasty Boys ~June 24, 1995
> - WCW World Television: **The Renegade** (won from Arn Anderson at Great American Bash, 6/18/1995)
>
> This period is one of the most consequential in WCW history — it includes the debut of Monday Nitro and the beginning of the Monday Night Wars.

## 2. Major TV/PPV Events

### 2A. Bash at the Beach 1995 — July 16, 1995

- **PPV date:** July 16, 1995
- **Show:** WCW Bash at the Beach 1995
- **Venue:** The Beach (outdoor venue), Huntington Beach, California
- **What happened:**
  - **Main Event PPV:** Hulk Hogan (c) (w/ Jimmy Hart) def. Big Van Vader in a **Steel Cage Match** to retain the WCW World Heavyweight Championship (13:13–13:23). Hogan escaped the cage to win. **Dennis Rodman** was the special outside enforcer; he attacked Zodiac and Kevin Sullivan when they tried to interfere. Vader's final WCW PPV match.
  - Sting (c) def. Meng to retain WCW US Heavyweight Championship (15:28–15:31)
  - Renegade (c) (w/ Jimmy Hart) def. Paul Orndorff to retain WCW World Television Championship (6:12)
  - Randy Savage def. Ric Flair in a **Lifeguard Lumberjack Match** (13:55–13:56)
  - Diamond Dallas Page (w/ Diamond Doll & Maxx Muscle) def. Dave Sullivan (4:23)
  - Kamala (w/ The Taskmaster) def. Jim Duggan (6:06) — **Dungeon of Doom PPV debut**
  - Harlem Heat (c) (w/ Sister Sherri) def. The Nasty Boys and The Blue Bloods in a **Triangle Match** for WCW World Tag Team Championship (13:08)
  - **Dark/Main Event matches:** Johnny B. Badd def. Chris Kanyon (2:03); Road Warrior Hawk def. Mark Starr (1:25); Dick Slater & Bunkhouse Buck def. Marcus Bagwell & Alex Wright (3:27)
- **Why it mattered:**
  - Hogan def. Vader for the THIRD time at PPV without a clean win. Dennis Rodman's involvement was a celebrity attraction.
  - Dungeon of Doom made their PPV debut (Kamala).
  - The outdoor beach setting gave the event a unique vibe but middle matches were criticized as flat.
  - Post-match: Ric Flair and Arn Anderson attacked Vader for failing to win — setting up Vader vs. Horsemen feud.
  - Vader's final WCW PPV appearance.

### 2B. WCW Monday Nitro Debut — September 4, 1995

- **Air date:** September 4, 1995 (LIVE)
- **Show:** WCW Monday Nitro — Episode 1
- **Venue:** Mall of America, Bloomington, Minnesota
- **Network:** TNT
- **Rating:** 2.5 (WWF Raw was preempted by US Open tennis — no direct competition)
- **Attendance:** Not precisely reported (mall crowd)
- **Commentary:** Eric Bischoff, Bobby Heenan, Steve McMichael (debut as commentator)
- **What happened:**
  - **Dark match:** The American Males (Marcus Bagwell & Scotty Riggs) def. Bunkhouse Buck & Dick Slater (non-title)
  - Brian Pillman def. Jushin "Thunder" Liger (6:00–6:52)
  - Sting (c) (US Champion) vs. Ric Flair ended in **No Contest** (8:43) — **Lex Luger made his surprise WCW return** during this match, appearing from the crowd
  - Hulk Hogan (c) (w/ Jimmy Hart) def. Big Bubba Rogers to retain WCW World Heavyweight Championship (7:08)
  - **Post-main event:** Dungeon of Doom attacked Hogan. Lex Luger ran out to make the save. Hogan and Luger came face-to-face — nose to nose. Sting and Savage made peace. Hogan told Luger to "go back where he came from."
  - Gene Okerlund interviewed Hogan and Luger. Luger challenged Hogan for the title. Hogan agreed.
  - **Vader was notably ABSENT** from the debut — featured in the title video package but not present. He had been scheduled to face Hogan on September 11 but was replaced by Lex Luger.
- **Why it mattered:**
  - **This was the beginning of the Monday Night Wars.** WCW's flagship weekly TV show launched on TNT.
  - Lex Luger's surprise return was the biggest pop — he had wrestled his final WWF contractual match the night before and signed with WCW that morning.
  - The show was smartly scheduled when WWF Raw was preempted by the US Open.
  - Set the template: Hogan main events, star returns, Dungeon of Doom attacks.
  - The title video notably featured Big Van Vader as one of four featured stars — but he was already being written out.

## 3. Roster Moves / Debuts / Exits

### 3A. Lex Luger — Returns to WCW (September 4, 1995)

- **Date:** September 4, 1995
- **Person:** Lex Luger (Lawrence Pfohl)
- **Change:** Made surprise return on Nitro debut. Had just wrestled his final WWF contractual match on September 3; signed with WCW that morning.
- **Impact:** Immediately positioned as a top babyface and potential Hogan challenger. Lost to Hogan by DQ on Nitro #2. Replaced Vader on the WarGames team at Fall Brawl. Turned heel by attacking Savage and joining Dungeon of Doom at Halloween Havoc (October 29).
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCW_Monday_Nitro ("Luger had just wrestled a match for the WWF the previous evening; the match was his final contractual obligation with the company, and Luger signed with WCW the morning of his appearance")
  - https://www.wwe.com/videos/lex-luger-returns-to-wcw-nitro-sept-4-1995

### 3B. Big Van Vader — Departure from WCW (~August/September 1995)

- **Date:** ~August/September 1995 (exact date disputed — see below)
- **Person:** Big Van Vader (Leon White)
- **Change:** Left WCW following a backstage altercation with Paul Orndorff (who was working as a backstage agent). Vader was suspended and offered a six-month pay cut (reportedly ~$400,000). He refused and left for WWF.
- **Kayfabe explanation:** WCW said he "went AWOL" in the build to Fall Brawl/WarGames.
- **Actual details:** Vader arrived late to a venue (or was at a photoshoot that ran long). Orndorff confronted him. Vader open-palm slapped Orndorff to the ground. Orndorff got up and they brawled. Meng eventually pulled Vader off. Bischoff suspended Vader with a massive fine. Vader refused the fine and settled out of his contract. Debuted in WWF within months.
- **Confidence:** High (Vader himself detailed the incident in interviews; confirmed by ring announcer David Penzer who witnessed it).
- **[UNCONFIRMED — FLAG]:** The exact date of the incident is uncertain. Sources say "shortly after" Clash of the Champions XXXI (August 6, 1995) or around the Fall Brawl build (September). Vader himself says he was suspended with 6 years left on his contract. Most sources place it in August-September 1995 timeframe.

### 3C. The Giant (Paul Wight) — Vignettes Begin / Physical Debut Build (July–September 1995)

- **Date:** July 1995 (vignettes begin); September 1995 (physical attacks on Hogan)
- **Person:** Paul Wight (billed as "The Giant," claimed to be son of André the Giant)
- **Change:**
  - **July 1995:** Dungeon of Doom vignettes began airing, introducing The Giant as crashing through walls. Vignettes described as "etched in stone."
  - **~September 10, 1995 (TV taping / Nitro #3 area):** The Giant drove a monster truck over Hogan's Harley Davidson motorcycle on TV.
  - **September 17, 1995 (Fall Brawl):** After WarGames, The Giant attacked Hogan and injured his neck.
  - The Giant's in-ring debut would come at Halloween Havoc (October 29, 1995).
- **Impact:** Massive hype for a wrestler who had never had a single in-ring match. The "son of André" angle was an immediate hook.
- **Confidence:** High.

## 4. Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### 4A. Vader vs. Orndorff Backstage Incident

- **Date:** ~August/September 1995 (exact date uncertain)
- **People:** Big Van Vader, Paul Orndorff (backstage agent), Meng
- **What happened:** Vader arrived late to a venue (was at a photoshoot). Orndorff confronted him aggressively. Vader open-palm slapped Orndorff to the ground. Orndorff got up and they fought. Vader backed against a wall, crossed his arms, and took hits from Orndorff without retaliating. Meng pulled Vader off. Others broke it up.
- **Why it mattered:** Led to Vader's suspension and eventual departure from WCW. WCW said he "went AWOL." Lex Luger replaced him at Fall Brawl's WarGames. Vader signed with WWF.
- **Confidence level:** High (confirmed by Vader, Orndorff, and eyewitness David Penzer).
- **Source:**

### 4E. Kevin Sullivan Takes Over Creative

- **Date:** ~1995 (ongoing process, finalized in this period)
- **People:** Kevin Sullivan, Ric Flair
- **What happened:** After Flair quit as booker in April 1995 (then returned briefly), Kevin Sullivan consolidated creative control. By July–September 1995, Sullivan was the de facto lead booker, designing the Dungeon of Doom and the Giant push.
- **Why it mattered:** Sullivan's creative direction would define WCW through 1996, including the Dungeon of Doom, the Giant push, and the booking philosophy that prioritized Hogan over in-ring quality.
- **Confidence level:** High.
- **Source:**
  - https://culturecrossfire.com/wrestling/wcw-slamboree-95/ (Flair quit/reinstated)

## 5. Game Effect Suggestions

### Momentum
- **Hogan:** Still World Champion but increasingly stagnant. Three PPV wins over Vader, none clean. The Giant is being built as his next challenge. The motorcycle/monster truck angle is cartoonish.
- **Sting:** Stable as US Champion. Retained at Bash at the Beach and on early Nitro episodes. Solid babyface.
- **Savage:** Active but in a secondary role. Beat Flair at Bash at the Beach. Building toward Halloween Havoc.
- **Lex Luger:** RISING FAST. Surprise return on Nitro debut was a massive pop. Immediately positioned as top contender.
- **The Giant:** MASSIVE BUILD. Hasn't had a single match but is being positioned as Hogan's ultimate challenger.
- **Diamond Dallas Page:** UP. Won TV Title at Fall Brawl — his first championship.
- **Arn Anderson:** UP. Beat Flair at Fall Brawl, declared New Horsemen with Pillman.
- **Brian Pillman:** UP. Part of New Horsemen with Anderson. Opened first Nitro.
- **Eddie Guerrero:** Debuting but injured.
- **Vader:** EFFECTIVELY GONE. Left the company.
- **Renegade:** DOWN. Lost TV Title at Fall Brawl. Push cratering.
- **Harlem Heat:** Active, won tag titles at Fall Brawl but lost them next night.

### Money
- **Nitro debut rating: 2.5** — solid for a new show, especially with Raw preempted. This began the Monday Night Wars.
- **PPV buyrates:**
  - Bash at the Beach: ~0.5 (estimated; no exact figure found but similar era events)
  - Fall Brawl: not precisely reported but middling
- **Nitro as a new TNT property** represented a major financial commitment from Turner.
- **Merchandise:** Hogan still the top draw but the Giant was being positioned for massive merch potential.

### Morale
- **Mixed-to-positive.** Luger's return was a genuine morale boost. Nitro's launch generated excitement. But Vader's departure, the rapid tag title changes, and the cartoonish Giant angle frustrated many.
- **The "New Horsemen" angle** gave Anderson and Pillman something fresh.
- **Eddie Guerrero and Disco Inferno debuts** brought fresh energy.

### Network Health
- **MAJOR CHANGE: Monday Nitro now airing on TNT.** This is the biggest network development of 1995.
- **The Monday Night Wars have begun.** WCW vs. WWF head-to-head.
- TBS still carries Saturday Night and Worldwide.
- TNT carries Nitro — a major upgrade in visibility.

### Fan Trust
- **Mixed.** Nitro's debut was well-received. Luger's return was a huge pop. But:
  - The Giant's build (no matches, just vignettes and monster trucks) tested patience.
  - Rapid tag title changes (4 changes in ~3 months) devalued the belts.
  - The Renegade as TV Champion was a credibility issue.
  - Hogan never losing cleanly continued to erode tension.

### Title Prestige
- **World Title (Hogan):** Declining but still the top prize.
- **US Title (Sting):** Stable. Sting is a credible champion.
- **TV Title:** NOW RISING. DDP winning it was an upgrade over Renegade.
- **Tag Titles:** CRATERED by rapid changes. Four changes in ~3 months (Harlem Heat → Buck/Slater → Harlem Heat → American Males → Harlem Heat).
