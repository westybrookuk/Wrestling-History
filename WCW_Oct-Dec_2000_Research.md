# WCW October-December 2000 Research Report

**Period Covered:** October 1, 2000 - December 31, 2000

## Executive Summary

The October-December 2000 period saw the final chapter of WCW unfold. Vince Russo vacated the World Title on October 2, and Booker T won it back in the absurd "San Francisco 49ers Match" against Jeff Jarrett. Scott Steiner's rise to the main event culminated in him winning the World Title at Mayhem (November 26) in a Straitjacket Steel Cage match, beginning his 120-day reign as the final dominant WCW World Champion. Halloween Havoc (October 29) and Starrcade (December 17) were the final PPVs in those franchises' histories. The Insiders (Kevin Nash & Diamond Dallas Page) formed and won the Tag Team Titles at Mayhem. Lance Storm continued his Team Canada angle but lost the US Title to General Rection at Halloween Havoc. WCW was hemorrhaging approximately $62 million in 2000. Eric Bischoff's Fusient Media Ventures made a $48.3 million bid in January 2001, but the deal collapsed when Jamie Kellner cancelled all WCW programming on TBS/TNT. WWF purchased WCW's assets for a staggeringly low $2.5 million (plus $1.7 million for the tape library) in March 2001. This period covers the last stable championship reigns before the company's terminal collapse.

## Title Changes

### WCW World Heavyweight Championship

**October 2, 2000 - Vince Russo to Vacated**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Cow Palace, Daly City/San Francisco, CA)
- **Result:** Vince Russo relinquished the title, stating he was not an athlete
- **Notes:** Russo had won the title on September 25 when Goldberg speared him through a cage. On Thunder (September 27), Russo declared himself champion. On October 2 Nitro, Russo formally vacated the title. He was transported to the arena in a glass-enclosed truck "like the Pope." Russo told Goldberg that if he lost one match, he'd be out of WCW; if he surpassed his 176-0 winning streak, he'd get a title shot.
- **Source:** https://pwwew.net/tv/nitro/001002.htm

**October 2, 2000 - Vacated to Booker T**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro (Cow Palace, Daly City/San Francisco, CA)
- **Match Type:** San Francisco 49ers Match (Booker T vs. Jeff Jarrett)
- **Result:** Booker T defeated Jeff Jarrett (13:02)
- **Notes:** Four boxes were hung over the ring corners - three contained weapons, one contained the title. Booker T won when Beetlejuice emerged from under the ring and hit Jarrett in the groin, allowing Booker to grab the box with the belt. Scott Steiner attacked Booker after the match, putting the Steiner Recliner on Beetlejuice. This was Booker T's third WCW World Heavyweight Championship reign. The match was well-received live but widely criticized as absurd on television.
- **Source:** https://prowrestling.fandom.com/wiki/October_2,_2000_Monday_Nitro_results

**November 26, 2000 - Booker T to Scott Steiner**
- **Event:** WCW Mayhem 2000 (U.S. Cellular Arena, Milwaukee, WI)
- **Match Type:** Straitjacket Caged Heat Match
- **Result:** Scott Steiner (w/ Midajah) defeated Booker T (c) by technical submission (13:10)
- **Notes:** Both men began restrained in straitjackets. After escaping, Steiner dominated with suplexes and hit Booker with a steel chair before applying the Steiner Recliner for the technical submission. This was Steiner's first WCW World Heavyweight Championship, making him a Triple Crown Champion. His 120-day reign would be the longest since Goldberg's 1998 reign, and he would be the final dominant champion before WCW's closure.
- **Attendance:** 3,800
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayhem_(2000)

### WCW's Sale to WWF

**Background:**
- WCW lost approximately $62 million in 2000
- AOL Time Warner wanted to sell WCW as quickly as possible
- Eric Bischoff's Fusient Media Ventures offered $48.3 million (including a $5 million deposit) in January 2001
- The Fusient deal collapsed when one of the major money backers pulled out
- Jamie Kellner (new head of TBS) cancelled all WCW programming on TBS and TNT
- Without a TV deal, Fusient withdrew their offer
- WWF's W. Acquisition Company purchased WCW's assets for $2.5 million in March 2001
- Vince McMahon also purchased the entire tape library for an additional $1.7 million
- Total sale price: $4.2 million
- The final WCW Nitro aired on March 26, 2001 (Panama City Beach, FL)
- **Source:** https://wrestlepedia.fandom.com/wiki/World_Championship_Wrestling

- **Lance Storm's US Title:** The Jul-Sep report noted Storm held the US Title through September. He lost it to General Rection at Halloween Havoc (October 29).
- **Tag Team Titles:** The Jul-Sep report noted Natural Born Thrillers won the vacant titles on September 25. This is consistent - they held them through October with the brief double-change on October 11.
- **KroniK's role:** The Jul-Sep report noted KroniK. They continued as a top tag team but lost to Goldberg at Halloween Havoc.
- **Goldberg:** The Jul-Sep report noted Goldberg's feud with Russo. Goldberg's "winning streak challenge" continued through October-December.
- **Bret Hart:** The Jul-Sep report noted Hart's final appearance was September 6. Hart did not appear during October-December.

## Conclusion

The October-December 2000 period covered WCW's final months as a functioning company. Vince Russo vacated the World Title on October 2, and Booker T won it back in the absurd "San Francisco 49ers Match." Scott Steiner emerged as the final dominant WCW World Champion at Mayhem (November 26), beginning a 120-day reign that briefly restored credibility to the title. The Insiders (Nash & DDP) formed and won the Tag Team Titles twice. Halloween Havoc (October 29) was the final event in that franchise's history, and Starrcade (December 17) was the last Starrcade. WCW was losing approximately $62 million in 2000. Eric Bischoff's Fusient Media Ventures made a $48.3 million bid in January 2001, but it collapsed when Jamie Kellner cancelled WCW programming. WWF purchased WCW's assets for just $4.2 million in March 2001. The final WCW Nitro aired on March 26, 2001. This period represents the quiet death of a promotion that had once dominated professional wrestling.
