## 2. Major TV, PPV, Supercard, and Arena Events

### ECW Guilty as Charged 2001 — January 7, 2001 (FINAL ECW PPV)

- **Date:** January 7, 2001
- **Venue:** Hammerstein Ballroom
- **City:** New York City, New York
- **Attendance:** 2,500
- **Network:** PPV
- **Commentary:** Joey Styles & Cyrus (Don Callis)
- **Main matches:**
  - Dark match: Bilvis Wesley defeated Mike Bell
  - Jerry Lynn & Cyrus defeated The Bad Street Boys (Christian York & Joey Matthews) (2:41)
  - ECW World Tag Team Championship: Danny Doring & Roadkill (c) defeated Hot Commodity (EZ Money & Julio Dinero) (w/ Chris Hamrick & Elektra) to retain the titles (10:06)
  - Nova defeated Chris Hamrick (w/ Elektra) (7:47) — Special referee: Spike Dudley
  - "I Quit" Match: Tommy Dreamer defeated CW Anderson (14:11)
  - #1 Contenders (ECW World Tag Team Championship) Three-Way Dance: The Unholy Alliance (Mikey Whipwreck & Yoshihiro Tajiri) (w/ The Sinister Minister) defeated The FBI (Little Guido & Tony Mamaluke) (w/ Sal E. Graziano) and Kid Kash & Super Crazy (13:31)
  - Simon Diamond & Johnny Swinger vs. Balls Mahoney & Chilly Willy ended in a No Contest (0:48)
  - ECW World Heavyweight Championship (Tables, Ladders, Chairs & Canes Match): The Sandman defeated Steve Corino (c) (w/ Jack Victory) and Justin Credible (w/ Francine/Dawn Marie) to win the title (13:20)
  - ECW World Heavyweight Championship: Rhino defeated The Sandman (c) to win the title (1:00-1:25)
  - ECW World Television Championship: Rob Van Dam defeated Jerry Lynn (w/ Cyrus) (19:30) — FINAL ECW PPV MATCH
- **Major finishes:**
  - The Sandman won the World title in a brutal TLC match, retrieving the title
  - Rhino immediately challenged and defeated an exhausted Sandman to become the FINAL ECW World Heavyweight Champion
  - Rob Van Dam defeated Jerry Lynn in the final ECW PPV match — a nearly 20-minute classic that ended ECW's PPV history
  - Tommy Dreamer defeated CW Anderson in an "I Quit" match
  - The Unholy Alliance won #1 contendership for tag titles
- **Important debuts/returns/exits:**
  - **Rhino became FINAL ECW World Heavyweight Champion** — set up dream match with RVD that never happened
  - **This was ECW's final PPV** — Guilty as Charged 2001 was the last nationally broadcast event
- **Why it mattered:** Guilty as Charged 2001 was ECW's final PPV and final nationally broadcast event. The show was surprisingly well-received given ECW's dire financial situation. Rhino became the final ECW World Champion. The RVD vs. Jerry Lynn main event was one of the better matches on the card. The show ended with RVD celebrating his victory, making it seem like ECW had a future — but the promotion would close just three months later. As Tommy Dreamer stated: "There was a big controversy based on money between Jerry Lynn, Rob Van Dam and Paul Heyman about doing that final show. Jerry didn't even bring his boots because he was owed so much money."

### ECW Bankruptcy — April 4, 2001

- **Date:** April 4, 2001
- **Filing:** H.H.G. Corporation (ECW's parent company) filed for bankruptcy in New Jersey
- **Financial details:**
  - **Assets:** $1,385,500 (personal bank account, leftover merchandise, video library, forthcoming TV revenue, production truck)
  - **Liabilities:** $8,881,435
  - **Net debt:** ~$7.5 million
- **Major creditors:**
  - **Rob Van Dam:** ~$150,000 owed
  - **Joey Styles:** $50,000+ owed
  - **Rhyno:** $50,000+ owed
  - **WWF:** $587,500 owed (WWF had been lending money to ECW)
  - **37 former ECW workers:** Amounts ranging from $300 to $150,000
  - **Steve Corino & Sabu:** $2 each owed (essentially nothing)
  - **In Demand (PPV distributor):** $3+ million owed
  - **Various venues, TV stations, production companies, travel agencies, phone companies, attorney's fees**
- **Why it mattered:** ECW's bankruptcy marked the official end of the promotion. The filing revealed the extent of ECW's financial ruin — the company owed nearly $9 million but had only $1.3 million in assets. Many wrestlers and staff were owed significant amounts of money. By filing for bankruptcy, Heyman was virtually absolved of the large debt. A bankruptcy judge would decide on the company's assets and determine how much, if anything, the creditors would collect. Many creditors would never see their money.

### Paul Heyman's Departure — February-March 2001

- **Date:** February-March 2001
- **What happened:** After ECW ceased operations, Paul Heyman joined WWF as a commentator on Raw Is War in February/March 2001, replacing Jerry Lawler (who had quit in protest after his then-wife Stacy Carter was fired). Heyman reportedly never told his wrestlers that ECW was on its last legs and was unable to pay them for a while.
- **Why it mattered:** Heyman's departure to WWF marked the end of his involvement with ECW. He had been the face of ECW since 1993 and was instrumental in building the promotion. His move to WWF as a commentator signaled that ECW was truly finished. Heyman would later become involved in the WWF Invasion storyline in July 2001, where he claimed to be bringing ECW back — but ECW was already dead.

## 5. Injuries, Deaths, Legal Issues, Financial Problems, and Backstage Controversies

### ECW Financial Collapse & Bankruptcy — April 4, 2001

- **People involved:** Paul Heyman, ECW management, 37+ creditors
- **Type:** Bankruptcy filing
- **What happened:** ECW's parent company H.H.G. Corporation filed for bankruptcy on April 4, 2001 in New Jersey. The company listed assets of $1,385,500 and liabilities of $8,881,435. ECW owed over $8 million in unpaid bills including salaries, TV deals, merchandise, PPV distributors, venues, and more. Rob Van Dam was owed ~$150,000; Joey Styles and Rhyno were each owed $50,000+; WWF was owed $587,500.
- **What was confirmed:** ECW filed for bankruptcy April 4, 2001; owed $8.8 million; had only $1.3 million in assets
- **What is disputed:** Whether Heyman could have saved ECW with different decisions
- **Immediate creative/business impact:** ECW ceased all operations; all talent released and mostly unpaid
- **Long-term impact:** WWF purchased ECW assets in January 2003; many creditors never saw their money
