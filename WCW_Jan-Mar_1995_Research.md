# WCW Research Report: January 1995 – End of March 1995

---

## 1. Title Changes

### 1A. WCW World Tag Team Championship — Stars & Stripes → Harlem Heat

- **Date:** January 8, 1995 (taping); aired January 21, 1995
- **Championship:** WCW World Tag Team Championship
- **Winners:** Harlem Heat (Booker T & Stevie Ray) (w/ Sister Sherri)
- **Losers:** Stars & Stripes (Marcus Alexander Bagwell & The Patriot)
- **Show:** WCW Saturday Night (taped at Center Stage Theatre, Atlanta, GA)
- **Details:** Booker T pinned the Patriot at 12:18 after the Heat Bomb. Booker T was the illegal man and crawled in behind the referee's back to make the pin.
- **Taping vs. air date:** Taped January 8, 1995. Aired January 21, 1995 on WCW Saturday Night (WCW).
- **Source:** thehistoryofwwe.com WCW Saturday Night 1995 results; wrestlingfans.fandom.com WCW Results 1995; WrestlePedia WCW Saturday Night Results 1995.

### 1B. No other confirmed title changes during Jan–Mar 1995

- **WCW World Heavyweight Championship:** Hulk Hogan (held throughout; won 7/17/1994)
- **WCW United States Heavyweight Championship:** Vader (held throughout this period; stripped in late April 1995 for "multiple violent offenses" — specifically the attack on Dave Sullivan)
- **WCW World Television Championship:** Arn Anderson (held throughout; retained at Clash XXX on 1/25/95 vs. Johnny B. Badd)

**Note:** Vader was stripped of the US Title on approximately April 23, 1995 (per prowestlingfandom.com), just outside this window, for the attack on Dave Sullivan and other incidents. A US Title tournament was then organized, with Sting winning the vacant title at The Great American Bash on June 18, 1995.

## 2. Major TV/PPV Events

### 2A. Clash of the Champions XXX — January 25, 1995

- **Date:** January 25, 1995
- **Show:** WCW Clash of the Champions XXX (20th Anniversary edition of the Clash series)
- **Venue:** Caesar's Palace, Las Vegas, Nevada
- **Attendance:** ~3,200–3,500 ($21,000 gate; 2,300 paid)
- **Network:** TBS (aired live; rating approximately 3.5)
- **What happened:**
  1. Brad & Scott Armstrong defeated Dick Slater & Bunkhouse Buck (8:32)
  2. **WCW TV Title:** Arn Anderson (c) (w/ Col. Parker & Meng) pinned Johnny B. Badd (7:38)
  3. Alex Wright pinned Bobby Eaton (7:38)
  4. **WCW Tag Team Title:** Harlem Heat (Booker T & Stevie Ray) (c) (w/ Sister Sherri) defeated Stars & Stripes (Marcus Alexander Bagwell & The Patriot) (9:45)
  5. Sting defeated Avalanche by submission (Scorpion Deathlock) — Special Guest Referee: The Guardian Angel (5:22). Guardian Angel turned heel post-match: after the match, Angel attacked Sting, ripping off his shirt to reveal a new look. He had been aligned with Avalanche/Kevin Sullivan all along. Big Bubba Rogers was reborn.
  6. **Main Event:** Hulk Hogan & Randy Savage (w/ Jimmy Hart) defeated Kevin Sullivan & The Butcher (11:04). Post-match, Vader confronted Hogan — hit him with a powerbomb. Flair appeared at ringside. Sting and Savage came out to clear the ring. Vader and Flair high-fived.
- **Why it mattered:**
  - Marked the first appearance of Ric Flair since his "retirement." He was still technically retired but began interfering in matches.
  - Guardian Angel turned heel, revealing he was aligned with Sullivan/Avalanche all along, rebranding as Big Bubba Rogers.
  - Set up the Hogan-Vader SuperBrawl match and the Flair/Vader alliance.
  - Confirmed Harlem Heat as tag champions (title change had already happened at the 1/8 taping).
- **Source:** prowestlinghistory.com Clash results; thehistoryofwwe.com WCW 1995 results; Wikipedia (SuperBrawl V article context); WrestlingFans Fandom; WrestlingRecaps review.

### 2B. SuperBrawl V — February 19, 1995

- **Date:** February 19, 1995
- **Show:** WCW SuperBrawl V (PPV)
- **Venue:** Baltimore Arena, Baltimore, Maryland
- **Attendance:** 13,390
- **Network:** TBS (PPV broadcast)
- **What happened:**
  - **Main Event PPV:** Hulk Hogan (c) (w/ Jimmy Hart) defeated Big Van Vader by DQ to retain the WCW World Heavyweight Championship (15:09–15:10). Ric Flair interfered during the match, attacking Hogan. The referee was knocked out; Vader hit the Vader Bomb. Flair attacked Hogan, resulting in a DQ. Post-match, Vader and Flair double-teamed Hogan until Sting and Savage made the save.
  - Sting & Randy Savage defeated Avalanche & Big Bubba Rogers (10:18)
  - Harlem Heat (c) (w/ Sister Sherri) defeated The Nasty Boys by DQ to retain the WCW World Tag Team Championship (17:07)
  - The Blacktop Bully (w/ Col. Robert Parker) defeated Dustin Rhodes (16:10)
  - Kevin Sullivan (w/ The Butcher) defeated Dave Sullivan (7:18)
  - Jim Duggan defeated Bunkhouse Buck (w/ Col. Parker & Meng) (11:58)
  - Alex Wright defeated Paul Roma (13:21)
  - **Main Event Dark:** Paul Orndorff defeated Brad Armstrong; Stars & Stripes defeated Valentino & Casanova; Arn Anderson (c) retained TV Title vs. Johnny B. Badd (lumberjack match)
- **Why it mattered:**
  - Hogan retained but by DQ again, not a clean finish — fans grew frustrated with the lack of decisive outcomes.
  - Ric Flair was physically involved, further establishing the Vader/Flair alliance against Hogan.
  - Kevin Sullivan vs. Dave Sullivan family feud continued (kayfabe).
  - The Blacktop Bully emerged as a major heel, defeating Dustin Rhodes cleanly.
  - Vader was presented as dominant but never cleanly beat Hogan.
- **Source:** Wikipedia (SuperBrawl V); thesmackdownhotel.com; prowestling.fandom.com; profightdb.com; NZPWI throwback review.

### 2C. Uncensored (Inaugural PPV) — March 19, 1995

- **Date:** March 19, 1995
- **Show:** WCW Uncensored (first-ever edition; PPV)
- **Venue:** Tupelo Coliseum, Tupelo, Mississippi
- **Attendance:** 5,782
- **PPV Rating:** 0.96 buyrate
- **Network:** PPV (live)
- **What happened:**
  - **Main Event PPV:** Hulk Hogan (w/ The Renegade) defeated Vader (w/ Ric Flair) in a Leather Strap Match (15:28–18:21). Jimmy Hart had been "kidnapped" by Vader and Flair but escaped during the match. A masked man attacked The Renegade. After the match, the masked man was revealed as Randy Savage (who had swapped places with Arn Anderson).
  - The Nasty Boys defeated Harlem Heat in a Falls Count Anywhere/Texas Tornado Match (8:43)
  - Big Bubba Rogers defeated Sting (13:43)
  - Randy Savage defeated Avalanche by DQ (11:44) — Ric Flair interfered dressed in drag/women's clothing. Notable: match was billed as "no DQ" but ended in DQ.
  - Johnny B. Badd defeated Arn Anderson in a Boxer vs. Wrestler match (knockout, 4th round)
  - Meng defeated Jim Duggan in a Martial Arts Match (7:04) — Special referee: Sonny Onoo
  - The Blacktop Bully defeated Dustin Rhodes in a King of the Road Match (13:06)
  - **Dark/Pre-show:** Alex Wright pinned Mark Starr (2:44); Steve Austin pinned Tim Horner (1:27); Stars & Stripes defeated Slater & Buck (9:34)
- **Why it mattered:**
  - **The Renegade debuted** as Hogan's "Ultimate Surprise" — a clear Ultimate Warrior clone gimmick played by Rick Wilson (Richard Wilson). Hogan teased for weeks that Warrior was coming to WCW; instead, it was Renegade. Managed by Jimmy Hart.
  - Inaugural "no DQ" PPV concept, though ironically the Savage match ended in DQ.
  - Hogan beat Vader but again without a clean finish — Flair was dragged around by the strap.
  - The Blacktop Bully was firmly established as a major heel under Col. Robert Parker.
  - Big Bubba Rogers beating Sting cleanly was significant.
  - Low buyrate (0.96) suggested fan fatigue with the Hogan-centric main events.
- **Source:** Wikipedia (Uncensored 1995); prowestlinghistory.com; thesmackdownhotel.com; prowestling.fandom.com; pwwew.net; WrestlingRecaps review.

### 2D. Key TV Moments (January–March 1995)

- **January 7, 1995 (WCW Worldwide, aired):** Commissioner Nick Bockwinkel ordered a Sting vs. Avalanche rematch at Clash XXX and appointed The Guardian Angel as special referee.
- **January 14, 1995 (WCW Saturday Night, Ft. Hood, TX — 2,418 sellout):** Hogan vs. Vader non-title match — Hogan won by DQ. Vader and Flair double-teamed Hogan post-match. Flair appeared at ringside.
- **February 11, 1995 (WCW Saturday Night):** Gene Okerlund interviewed Ric Flair, who predicted Vader would win the title at SuperBrawl.
- **February 18, 1995 (WCW Saturday Night):** Flair announced he would appear at SuperBrawl during Hogan-Vader match.
- **Post-SuperBrawl (late Feb/early March):** Ric Flair was formally reinstated as a wrestler after months of Hogan and Savage petitioning WCW management. Flair was explained as having "nagged Hogan for months."
- **March house show tour (9th–12th):** Consistent card across El Paso, Amarillo, Salt Lake City, Denver. Vader won US Title defenses vs. Duggan; Blacktop Bully beat Dustin Rhodes; Hogan & Savage vs. Sullivan & Avalanche continued.
- **Early April (just outside window):** Vader and Flair attacked Dave Sullivan, leading to Vader being stripped of the US Title.

## 3. Roster Moves / Debuts / Exits

### 3A. The Renegade — Debut (March 19, 1995)

- **Date/Month:** March 19, 1995 (Uncensored PPV)
- **Person:** Rick Wilson (ring name: The Renegade)
- **Change:** Debuted as Hulk Hogan's "Ultimate Surprise" — a face-painted, high-energy character patterned closely on The Ultimate Warrior. Managed by Jimmy Hart.
- **Impact:** WCW had been trying to sign the real Ultimate Warrior but couldn't reach terms. Instead, they created a clone. Initial reaction was positive (crowd buzz, strong push) but once it was confirmed he was NOT Warrior, interest evaporated. The character was a critical and fan disaster. Wilson was named Wrestling Observer "Worst Wrestler" of 1995. The gimmick was eventually dropped; Wilson was reduced to enhancement talent before release. Tragically, Wilson committed suicide in February 1999 at age 33.
- **Confidence:** Confirmed fact.
- **Source:** Wikipedia (Renegade); thesportster.com; 411mania.com (Bischoff 83 Weeks podcast); grokipedia.com; wrestlingdeaths.com.

### 3B. Ric Flair — Reinstated as Active Wrestler (Late February/March 1995)

- **Date/Month:** Late February / early March 1995
- **Person:** Ric Flair
- **Change:** Returned to active in-ring competition after his "retirement." First physical involvement was at SuperBrawl V (February 19) interfering in Hogan-Vader match. Formally reinstated as a wrestler shortly after, with storyline explanation that Hogan and Savage had petitioned WCW management to bring him back.
- **Impact:** Immediately formed an alliance with Vader to oppose Hogan. This led to the Four Horsemen reunion arc later in 1995. Flair began appearing regularly in matches and angles.
- **Confidence:** Confirmed fact.
- **Source:** Wikipedia (SuperBrawl V, Ric Flair); WrestlingRecaps; culturecrossfire.com (Slamboree 1995 recap).

### 3C. The Guardian Angel → Big Bubba Rogers (January 25, 1995)

- **Date/Month:** January 25, 1995 (Clash of the Champions XXX)
- **Person:** Ray Traylor (Guardian Angel → Big Bubba Rogers)
- **Change:** The Guardian Angel, who had been appointed as special referee for Sting vs. Avalanche, turned heel post-match by attacking Sting. He was revealed to have been aligned with Kevin Sullivan and Avalanche all along. Rebranded as "Big Bubba Rogers" (essentially his previous Big Bubba/Big Boss Man character repackaged).
- **Impact:** Established as a major heel enforcer under Col. Robert Parker's management. Beat Sting cleanly at Uncensored. Became a recurring threat in the Hogan era.
- **Confidence:** Confirmed fact.
- **Source:** prowestlinghistory.com Clash results; chrononaut79 LiveJournal recap of Clash XXX; Wikipedia.

### 3D. The Blacktop Bully — Pushed as Major Heel (January–March 1995)

- **Date/Month:** January–March 1995
- **Person:** The Blacktop Bully (w/ Col. Robert Parker)
- **Change:** Received a significant push during this period. Won King of the Road match vs. Dustin Rhodes at Uncensored. Previously a mid-card talent, now positioned as a top heel.
- **Impact:** Became a legit threat on TV, defeating established names. Part of Col. Robert Parker's "stud" stable.
- **Confidence:** Confirmed fact.
- **Source:** Multiple results databases; Uncensored 1995 results.

### 3E. Harley Race — Departure (January 1995)

- **Date/Month:** January 1995
- **Person:** Harley Race
- **Change:** Left WCW after a real-life automobile accident. Had been managing Vader.
- **Impact:** Vader lost his manager. This was the kayfabe explanation for Vader being without management heading into his feud with Hogan. Race's departure was a legitimate injury, not a creative decision.
- **Confidence:** Confirmed fact.
- **Source:** Wikipedia (Big Van Vader); culturecrossfire.com Slamboree recap.

### 3F. Steve Austin — Returning from Injury (Early 1995)

- **Date/Month:** February 1995 (approximate)
- **Person:** "Stunning" Steve Austin
- **Change:** Returned from a serious knee injury (two torn posterior ligaments, suffered late 1994). Returned after approximately 2.5 months on the shelf. In his first match back, suffered a cut requiring 12 stitches near his eye in a mishap working with Marcus Bagwell.
- **Impact:** Austin was back but not yet prominently featured in this specific Jan-Mar window on major TV. He wrestled on the Uncensored pre-show (pinning Tim Horner in 1:27). He would go to Japan in May 1995 and tear his triceps, leading to his eventual firing in September 1995.
- **Confidence:** Confirmed fact (from Wrestling Observer and multiple sources).
- **Source:** Wikipedia (Steve Austin); cultaholic.com; thesportster.com; reddit r/TheDirtsheets (Wrestling Observer July-Dec 1995 excerpts).

### 3G. Diamond Dallas Page — Early WCW Run (January–March 1995)

- **Date/Month:** January–March 1995 (ongoing)
- **Person:** Diamond Dallas Page (w/ The Diamond Doll/Kimberly and Maxx Muscle)
- **Change:** DDP was active on the house show circuit and TV tapings during this period, losing to Alex Wright and others. His gimmick (a flashy, fitness-obsessed character) was being developed. Kimberly (his real-life wife) served as "The Diamond Doll" valet.
- **Impact:** Still being positioned as a mid-card heel at this point. Would get more prominent later in 1995.
- **Confidence:** Confirmed fact.
- **Source:** wrestlingfans.fandom.com; thesmackdownhotel.com.

## 4. Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### 4A. Ric Flair's "Retirement" Charade and Return

- **Date/Month:** January–February 1995
- **People involved:** Ric Flair, Hulk Hogan, Randy Savage, WCW management
- **What happened:** Flair had been "retired" since losing to Hogan at Fall Brawl 1994 (October). He made appearances at ringside starting at Clash XXX (January 25), then physically interfered at SuperBrawl V (February 19). The storyline explanation was that Hogan and Savage had been nagging WCW to reinstate Flair, which they did. In reality, Flair was always going to return; the "retirement" was a storyline to build heat.
- **Why it mattered:** The extended retirement angle frustrated fans who wanted to see Flair wrestle. When he returned, he immediately aligned with Vader — a surprising heel team given their bitter history. This began the slow rebuild of the Four Horsemen.
- **Confidence level:** High.
- **Source:** WrestlingRecaps; culturecrossfire.com; Wikipedia.

### 4B. Vader's US Title Behavior and Eventual Stripping

- **Date/Month:** March 1995 (build); April 23, 1995 (stripped)
- **People involved:** Big Van Vader, Ric Flair, Dave Sullivan (Evad Sullivan)
- **What happened:** During January–March 1995, Vader treated the US Title as an afterthought, barely defending it while focusing on his feud with Hogan. In March, Vader and Flair attacked Dave Sullivan (kayfabe brother of Kevin/Dave Sullivan). This "last straw" attack was used as the kayfabe reason to strip Vader of the US Title. The actual stripping happened around April 23, 1995.
- **Why it mattered:** This was part of a pattern of WCW not protecting its secondary title. The US Title became essentially a prop during Vader's reign. The stripping led to a tournament eventually won by Sting at The Great American Bash.
- **Confidence level:** High.
- **Source:** Wikipedia (Great American Bash 1995, Big Van Vader); culturecrossfire.com; thesportster.com.

### 4C. Kevin Sullivan's Kayfabe Family Feud

- **Date/Month:** Ongoing January–March 1995
- **People involved:** Kevin Sullivan, Dave Sullivan, The Butcher/Avalanche
- **What happened:** The Sullivan "family" angle continued — Kevin Sullivan (as the villainous Taskmaster figure) feuding with his kayfabe brother Dave Sullivan. At SuperBrawl V, Kevin Sullivan defeated Dave Sullivan.
- **Why it mattered:** This was one of the more bizarre kayfabe angles of the era, with real-life friends/brothers playing enemies. Dave Sullivan was being pushed as a babyface despite limited in-ring ability.
- **Confidence level:** High.
- **Source:** SuperBrawl V results; multiple databases.

### 4D. The Renegade Gimmick Controversy

- **Date/Month:** March 19, 1995 (debut); aftermath through 1995
- **People involved:** Rick Wilson (The Renegade), Ultimate Warrior (Jim Hellwig), WCW, WWF
- **What happened:** WCW spent weeks teasing that The Ultimate Warrior was coming to WCW as Hogan's "Ultimate Surprise." At Uncensored, they revealed it was actually The Renegade — a clear Warrior clone with face paint, tassels, and mannerisms. When the real Warrior publicly denied any involvement, fans lost interest. WWF reportedly sent a legal notice over the stolen gimmick, and WCW later had to remove The Renegade's tassels and stop him from shaking the ropes.
- **Why it mattered:** This was a significant credibility blow. It demonstrated WCW's willingness to use ripoffs rather than secure original talent. It would set a pattern for future creative decisions.
- **Confidence level:** High (confirmed by Bischoff himself on 83 Weeks podcast).
- **Source:** 411mania.com (Bischoff 83 Weeks); thesportster.com; Wikipedia (Renegade); culturecrossfire.com.

- **People involved:** Brian Pillman, Marcus Alexander Bagwell
- **What happened:** Steve Austin, in his first match back from knee surgery, had a mishap working with Marcus Bagwell. Austin required 12 stitches near his eye. Note: Some sources attribute this to Pillman rather than Austin; this may have been a separate incident. [UNCERTAIN — flagging for verification]

### 4F. Road Warrior Animal — Back Injury (Ongoing)

- **Date/Month:** Ongoing through early-mid 1995

## 5. Game Effect Suggestions

### Momentum
- **Hogan:** Stagnant. Retained the World Title at both SuperBrawl V and Uncensored but never cleanly. DQ finishes and interference-heavy matches were becoming predictable. Fans knew Hogan wouldn't lose cleanly, creating a "why bother" feeling.
- **Vader:** Declining. Despite being presented as a monster, he never beat Hogan cleanly. Three PPV matches (SuperBrawl, Uncensored, and later Bash at the Beach) with no decisive victory made him look weak. His US Title reign was treated as an afterthought.
- **Sting:** Stable but underutilized on PPV. Lost to Big Bubba at Uncensored (non-title). His feud with Avalanche/Guardian Angel concluded at Clash XXX. He was positioned for a US Title tournament run.
- **Savage:** Solid but inconsistent. Flair in drag interfering at Uncensored was bizarre and damaged the "no DQ" concept.
- **Renegade:** Initial debut pop, but the Warrior clone gimmick would crater quickly. A dead end.
- **Harlem Heat:** Strong — won the tag titles and held them firmly. Sister Sherri was effective as manager.
- **Arn Anderson:** Solid as TV champion but being protected in short matches.
- **Blacktop Bully / Big Bubba Rogers:** Rising heels — both pushing up the card under Col. Parker.

### Money
- **PPV buyrates declining:** Uncensored did a 0.96 buyrate. SuperBrawl V (no exact figure found but middling). The constant Hogan-centric main events without clean finishes were hurting draws.
- **Hogan still the top merchandise draw** but the Hogan-centric product was wearing thin with the hardcore fanbase.
- **Live events** were still strong — the Ft. Hood show drew a sellout (2,418). SuperBrawl V in Baltimore drew 13,390.
- **WCW had not yet launched Monday Nitro** (that came September 4, 1995), so TV revenue was still based on TBS/WTBS programming (Saturday Night, Worldwide, Pro).

### Morale
- **Mixed.** Flair's reinstatement pleased some wrestlers but the constant Hogan-centric booking frustrated many. The Renegade gimmick was embarrassing to many in the locker room. Steve Austin was still around but would be sent to Japan and ultimately fired by mid-1995. Ric Flair was part-time booker, which caused friction.
- **Kevin Sullivan** was consolidating creative power (he would become lead booker after Flair quit in April 1995).
- **Jim Barnett and other WCW officials** were fired in cost-cutting moves around this time (per culturecrossfire.com), as Ted Turner wanted to buy more stations.

### Network Health
- **TBS/WTBS:** Still WCW's primary platform. Saturday Night and Worldwide were the main TV shows. The network was profitable but WCW itself was not yet turning a profit.
- **No Monday Nitro yet** — that was still ~8 months away (September 4, 1995).
- **PPV distribution** was standard cable/satellite. No major network deals for PPV.

### Fan Trust
- **Eroding.** The Renegade as a Warrior clone was a major trust issue — fans felt deceived.
- **Hogan's DQ finishes** were becoming tedious — fans knew he'd never lose cleanly, so matches had no tension.
- **The Guardian Angel/Big Bubba switch** was received as a decent heel turn but the angle was somewhat confusing.
- **Uncensored as a concept** (no DQ PPV) was undermined when the Savage match ended in DQ. Fans noticed.
- **Hardcore fans** (Observer readers, etc.) were increasingly frustrated with WCW's booking while WWF was building momentum with Diesel, Shawn Michaels, Bret Hart, and the ladder matches.

### Title Prestige
- **World Title (Hogan):** Declining. Never lost cleanly in this period. DQ finishes made the belt feel less important.
- **US Title (Vader):** Near-zero prestige. Vader barely defended it, and it was treated as a side note while he chased the World Title.
- **TV Title (Arn Anderson):** Stable. Arn was a credible champion who defended regularly and put on good matches (e.g., the lumberjack match vs. Badd at SuperBrawl).
- **Tag Titles (Harlem Heat):** Rising. The Heat were an over team and Sister Sherri added heat. Their feud with the Nasty Boys was solid.

### Roster Availability
- **Hogan:** Available, main eventing every PPV
- **Savage:** Available, co-main eventing
- **Sting:** Available
- **Vader:** Available, being featured prominently
- **Ric Flair:** Now active (returned late Feb/March)
- **Lex Luger:** Not yet in WCW (was still in WWF/undecided; signed with WCW for Nitro debut in September)
- **Road Warrior Animal:** Out with back injury
- **Road Warrior Hawk:** Active (singles and tag work)
- **Steve Austin:** Back from knee injury, active on house shows/pre-shows; would go to Japan in May, tear triceps, be fired in September
- **Arn Anderson:** Active, TV champion
- **Brian Pillman:** Active
- **The Giant:** Not yet debuted (would appear July 1995; was teased at Slamboree in May — visible at ringside during Hogan/Savage entrance)
- **Diamond Dallas Page:** Active, mid-card
- **Chris Benoit:** Active (appeared on Collision in Korea tour in April)
- **Eddie Guerrero:** Active on house shows/TV tapings
- **Harlem Heat (Booker T & Stevie Ray):** Active, tag champions
- **The Nasty Boys:** Active, top tag challengers
- **Dustin Rhodes:** Active, losing to rising heels
- **Jim Duggan:** Active, mid-card babyface

## Summary: Key Narrative Threads During This Period

1. **Hogan vs. Vader:** The dominant angle of Jan–Mar 1995. Three PPV encounters with no clean finish. Ric Flair's interference made Vader look weak. This would culminate in a steel cage match at Bash at the Beach (July 1995).

2. **Flair's Return & Horsemen Rebuild:** Ric Flair went from "retired" to active wrestler, immediately aligning with Vader. This set up the Four Horsemen reunion with Arn Anderson later in 1995.

3. **The Renegade Debacle:** WCW's failed attempt to create a Warrior clone. A low point in creative.

4. **Harlem Heat's Tag Dominance:** Won titles in January, successfully defended through this period. Solid workhorse angle.

5. **Col. Parker's Stud Stable:** Big Bubba Rogers, Blacktop Bully, and Meng all pushed as threats. Parker was an effective manager.

6. **Sting's Transition:** After the Guardian Angel/Avalanche angle ended, Sting was being set up for the US Title tournament (which would happen at Great American Bash in June).

7. **The Pre-Nitro Era:** WCW was still running its standard Saturday Night/Worldwide/Pro TV structure. Monday Nitro was still months away but in development.
