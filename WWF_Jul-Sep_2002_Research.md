# WWF July-September 2002 Research Report

**Period Covered:** July 1, 2002 - September 30, 2002

## Executive Summary

The July-September 2002 period marked the full implementation of the Brand Extension era, featuring three major PPV events — Vengeance (July 21, Detroit), SummerSlam (August 25, Uniondale), and Unforgiven (September 22, Los Angeles) — and marked by Brock Lesnar winning the WWE Championship from The Rock at SummerSlam to become the youngest WWE Champion in history at age 25, The Rock winning the WWE Undisputed Championship at Vengeance in a Triple Threat match, and Brock Lesnar retaining the WWE Championship in a double disqualification match against The Undertaker at Unforgiven. Key developments included: the Brand Extension being fully implemented with Raw and SmackDown becoming separate brands; Brock Lesnar defeating The Rock at SummerSlam to become the youngest WWE Champion in history; Shawn Michaels defeating Triple H in an Unsanctioned Street Fight at SummerSlam in his first match since WrestleMania XIV; The Rock winning the WWE Undisputed Championship at Vengeance in a Triple Threat match also involving Kurt Angle and The Undertaker; and Brock Lesnar retaining the WWE Championship in a double disqualification match against The Undertaker at Unforgiven. Vengeance drew 12,000 attendance at the Joe Louis Arena in Detroit. SummerSlam drew 16,000 attendance at the Nassau Veterans Memorial Coliseum in Uniondale. Unforgiven drew 16,000 attendance at the Staples Center in Los Angeles with 0.57 buyrate.

**Brand Status:** Brand Extension fully implemented

- **Authority Figures:**
  - **Raw:** Eric Bischoff (General Manager)
  - **SmackDown:** Stephanie McMahon (General Manager)
- **Title Exclusivity:**
  - **WWE Championship:** SmackDown exclusive (after Brock Lesnar won at SummerSlam)
  - **World Heavyweight Championship:** Raw exclusive (created September 2, 2002)
  - **WWE Intercontinental Championship:** Raw exclusive

  - **WWE Tag Team Championship:** SmackDown exclusive
  - **WWE Women's Championship:** Raw exclusive

  - **WWE Hardcore Championship:** SmackDown exclusive (until unified with Intercontinental Championship)

**World Heavyweight Championship Creation:**
- **Date:** September 2, 2002
- **Reason:** WWE Championship became exclusive to SmackDown after Brock Lesnar won at SummerSlam
- **First Champion:** Triple H (awarded by Eric Bischoff)
- **Brand:** Raw exclusive

## Conclusion

The July-September 2002 period marked the full implementation of the Brand Extension era, featuring three major PPV events and major storylines. The Brand Extension was fully implemented, splitting the WWF roster into Raw and SmackDown brands. Brock Lesnar defeated The Rock at SummerSlam to win the WWE Championship and become the youngest WWE Champion in history at age 25. The World Heavyweight Championship was created for the Raw brand on September 2, 2002, with Triple H as the first champion. The Rock won the WWE Undisputed Championship at Vengeance in a Triple Threat match also involving Kurt Angle and The Undertaker. Shawn Michaels defeated Triple H in an Unsanctioned Street Fight at SummerSlam in his first match since WrestleMania XIV. Rob Van Dam won the WWE Intercontinental Championship from Chris Benoit on Raw (July 29), then lost to Chris Benoit at SummerSlam. The Un-Americans (Christian & Lance Storm) won the WWE Tag Team Championship at Vengeance. Trish Stratus won the WWE Women's Championship from Molly Holly at Unforgiven. Brock Lesnar retained the WWE Championship in a double disqualification match against The Undertaker at Unforgiven. Vengeance drew 12,000 attendance at the Joe Louis Arena in Detroit. SummerSlam drew 16,000 attendance at the Nassau Veterans Memorial Coliseum in Uniondale. Unforgiven drew 16,000 attendance at the Staples Center in Los Angeles with 0.57 buyrate.
