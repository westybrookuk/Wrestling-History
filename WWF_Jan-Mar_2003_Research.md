# WWF January-March 2003 Research Report

**Period Covered:** January 1, 2003 - March 31, 2003

## Executive Summary

The January-March 2003 period marked one of the most significant quarters in WWE history, featuring three major PPV events — Royal Rumble (January 19, Boston), No Way Out (February 23, Montreal), and WrestleMania XIX (March 30, Seattle) — that set the stage for the Ruthless Aggression era. Key developments included: the formation of the dominant Evolution stable on January 20, 2003; Brock Lesnar winning the 2003 Royal Rumble match; "Stone Cold" Steve Austin's return at No Way Out; The Rock defeating Hulk Hogan in a controversial Montreal-style screwjob finish at No Way Out; and WrestleMania XIX drawing 54,097 fans and $2.76 million in gate revenue at Safeco Field in Seattle. Triple H retained the World Heavyweight Championship throughout the quarter, while Brock Lesnar won the WWE Championship from Kurt Angle in the WrestleMania XIX main event. The Rock defeated Stone Cold Steve Austin in their third and final WrestleMania match, Hulk Hogan defeated Vince McMahon in a Street Fight billed "20 Years in the Making," and The Undertaker extended his WrestleMania undefeated streak to 11-0. Royal Rumble drew 15,338 attendance with 585,000 PPV buys. No Way Out drew 15,100 attendance with 450,000 buys. WrestleMania XIX drew 54,097 attendance with 560,000 buys.

- First WrestleMania to feature defenses of both the WWE Championship and World Heavyweight Championship on the same card
- WrestleMania XIX drew 54,097 fans from all 50 states and numerous countries — largest crowd at Safeco Field
- $2.76 million gate revenue — record for Safeco Field; fans from all 50 US states attended
- The Undertaker's WrestleMania streak advanced to 11-0, defeating Big Show and A-Train in a handicap match
- Triple H retained World Heavyweight Championship with Ric Flair interference; Booker T injured his leg executing the Harlem Hangover, allowing Triple H to capitalize with a Pedigree (18:45/18:47); 15 seconds passed between the move and being able to make the cover

- The Rock defeated Steve Austin in their third and final WrestleMania match (rated 8/10 stars); Rock delivered three consecutive Rock Bottoms after Austin kicked out of an earlier attempt (17:53/17:55); Austin suffered a post-match Stunner from Rock; signified Austin's retirement from full-time in-ring competition

- Hulk Hogan defeated Vince McMahon in Street Fight billed "20 Years in the Making" after three leg drops (20:47/20:49); McMahon climbed a ladder and hit a leg drop on Hogan through the Spanish announce table; Sean O'Haire interfered late; Roddy Piper was also in Hogan's corner; stipulation: if Hogan lost, he would have had to retire from in-ring competition
- Trish Stratus won her second WWE Women's Championship in Triple Threat Match vs. Victoria and Jazz (7:17)
- Team Angle (Charlie Haas & Shelton Benjamin) retained WWE Tag Team Championship in Triple Threat Tag Team Match vs. Chris Benoit & Rhyno and Los Guerreros (8:46/8:48)
- Matt Hardy retained Cruiserweight Championship vs. Rey Mysterio (5:37/5:39) with Shannon Moore interference
- Limp Bizkit performed "Crack Addict" live and "Rollin' (Air Raid Vehicle)" during The Undertaker's entrance
- The event was presented by Snickers Cruncher
- Production featured innovative use of stadium's retractable roof to suspend lighting and sound rigging via 132 steel cables spanning 275 feet and rising over 115 feet high

## Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### Evolution Formation and Early Injuries
The Evolution stable formed on January 20, 2003, when Randy Orton (returning from shoulder injury) joined Triple H, Ric Flair, and Batista in attacking Scott Steiner. However, the group got off to a rocky start when both Orton and Batista were injured at a March 1, 2003 house show at the Sovereign Center in Reading, Pennsylvania. Orton injured his shoulder while Batista tore his right triceps. Batista would be sidelined for nearly 8 months after tearing and then re-tearing his triceps while rehabilitating.

### The No Way Out Screwjob in Montreal
The Rock vs. Hulk Hogan match at No Way Out in Montreal (February 23) ended in controversial fashion when the lights went out, referee Sylvain Grenier appeared knocked out, and Vince McMahon distracted Hogan, allowing Rock to use a steel chair for the win. After the match, McMahon revealed a "Hulkamania Sucks" t-shirt and tore it in Hogan's face. This was particularly controversial as Montreal was the site of the famous "Montreal Screwjob" at Survivor Series 1997 involving Bret Hart.

### Stone Cold Steve Austin's Return and Co-GM Role
Austin returned at No Way Out after 8 months away, defeating Eric Bischoff. On the February 24 episode of Raw, Bischoff "fired" Austin on medical grounds, but Linda McMahon brought him back as Co-General Manager of Raw. This was a way to keep Austin on television while limiting his in-ring performance due to long-running injuries. Austin and Bischoff continued to feud over control of the brand throughout March.

### Brock Lesnar's Royal Rumble Win
Brock Lesnar won the Royal Rumble from the #29 position, entering last and eliminating The Undertaker to win. This set up his WWE Championship match against Kurt Angle at WrestleMania XIX.

### Hulk Hogan's Return and Feuds
Hulk Hogan returned to WWE on January 23, 2003, after several years away. His return led to immediate feuds with both Vince McMahon and The Rock. The Rock-Hogan feud culminated at No Way Out with the controversial finish, while the McMahon-Hogan feud ended at WrestleMania XIX with Hogan winning a Street Fight.

## Conclusion

The January-March 2003 period was defined by the formation of Evolution, the returns of Stone Cold Steve Austin and Hulk Hogan, and the spectacular success of WrestleMania XIX. The quarter opened with the Royal Rumble, where Brock Lesnar's victory set up his WWE Championship win at WrestleMania. Evolution's formation on January 20 created one of the most dominant stables in WWE history, though early injuries to Batista and Randy Orton hampered the group initially.

No Way Out in Montreal was memorable for Stone Cold Steve Austin's return and the controversial Rock-Hogan match that ended in a screwjob finish — ironically in the same city as the famous Montreal Screwjob six years earlier. The quarter culminated with WrestleMania XIX in Seattle, which drew 54,097 fans and $2.76 million in gate revenue. The event featured both world championships being defended, with Brock Lesnar winning the WWE Championship from Kurt Angle in a critically acclaimed main event, while Triple H retained the World Heavyweight Championship. The Rock defeated Steve Austin in their third and final WrestleMania match, and Hulk Hogan defeated Vince McMahon in a Street Fight.

By the end of March 2003, the championship landscape was: Brock Lesnar (WWE Champion, SmackDown!), Triple H (World Heavyweight Champion, Raw), Lance Storm & Chief Morley (World Tag Team Champions, Raw), Team Angle (WWE Tag Team Champions, SmackDown!), Trish Stratus (Women's Champion, Raw), and Matt Hardy (Cruiserweight Champion, SmackDown!). Evolution was established as the dominant force on Raw, while the Rock-Austin feud would continue into the summer. The stage was set for the Ruthless Aggression era to fully take hold.
