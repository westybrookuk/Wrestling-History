# WCW January-March 2001 Research Report

**Period Covered:** January 1, 2001 - March 31, 2001

## Executive Summary

The January-March 2001 period marked the absolute final chapter of World Championship Wrestling. Scott Steiner dominated as WCW World Heavyweight Champion while leading Ric Flair's Magnificent Seven stable. Three final PPVs were held: Sin (January 14, Indianapolis), SuperBrawl Revenge (February 18, Nashville), and Greed (March 18, Jacksonville) - the last WCW PPV ever. Behind the scenes, Eric Bischoff's Fusient Media Ventures attempted to purchase WCW in January 2001, but the deal collapsed on March 19 when Jamie Kellner cancelled all WCW programming on TBS/TNT. On March 23, Vince McMahon's WWF purchased WCW's assets for approximately $7 million ($3 million for intangible properties plus additional amounts for the tape library and lawsuit settlements). The final WCW Thunder aired March 21, and the final WCW Nitro aired March 26 from Panama City Beach, Florida - "Night of Champions" where Booker T won the World Title from Scott Steiner, and Sting defeated Ric Flair in the last match in WCW history. The two legends embraced after the match, closing the book on WCW.

## Title Changes

### WCW World Heavyweight Championship

**[Scott Steiner held the title throughout this period, having won it from Booker T on November 26, 2000 at Mayhem. His 120-day reign ended on March 26, 2001.]**

**March 26, 2001 - Scott Steiner to Booker T**
- **Event:** WCW Monday Nitro - "Night of Champions" (Boardwalk Beach Resort, Panama City Beach, FL)
- **Match Type:** Title vs. Title Match (Steiner's World Title vs. Booker T's US Title)
- **Result:** Booker T (c) [US] defeated Scott Steiner (c) [World] to win the World Championship (5:11)
- **Notes:** The final WCW World Title change. Shane Douglas interfered, hitting Steiner with his cast, allowing Booker T to hit the Book End for the win. Booker T became a double champion, holding both the World and US Titles. He would take the WCW World Heavyweight Championship to WWF, where it was renamed the WCW Championship with Booker T as the inaugural champion under the WWF banner.

### WCW Hardcore Championship

**January 14, 2001 - Terry Funk to Meng**
- **Event:** WCW Sin 2001 (Indianapolis, IN)
- **Match Type:** Triple Threat Hardcore Match (Meng vs. Crowbar vs. Terry Funk (c))

## WCW's Final Days - Business Collapse

### Timeline of WCW's Collapse (January-March 2001)

**January 11, 2001:**
- Fusient Media Ventures (led by Eric Bischoff) announces press release declaring acquisition of WCW
- Deal valued at approximately $75 million with TBS holding minority stake
- Brian Bedol (co-founder of Classic Sports Network) set to become CEO
- Eric Bischoff granted full control immediately, begins running WCW television

**Mid-February 2001:**
- Fusient deal starts to unravel as money backers become aware of WCW's financial situation
- WCW had lost approximately $62 million in 2000 alone
- One major money backer pulls out

**February 18, 2001:**
- SuperBrawl Revenge takes place - penultimate WCW PPV
- Kevin Nash forced to leave WCW (worked into storyline)

**March 18, 2001:**
- WCW Greed - the final WCW PPV ever
- PPV buyrate of 50,000 - lowest in WCW history

**March 19, 2001:**
- **Jamie Kellner, new head of Turner Broadcasting System, cancels ALL WCW programming on TBS and TNT**
- Kellner believed wrestling did not fit the demographics of TBS/TNT
- This destroyed the Fusient Media Ventures deal (which depended on Turner broadcast slots)
- Bischoff later said: "That took the deal from something worth roughly $67 million to something worth $67. Maybe."

**March 21, 2001:**
- Final WCW Thunder airs

**March 23, 2001:**
- **Vince McMahon's WWF announces purchase of WCW**
- WWF purchases WCW's trademarks and archived video library for approximately $7 million ($3 million for intangible properties plus additional for tape library and lawsuit settlements)
- Only 24 contracts were included in the sale
- WCW's losses were written off via purchase accounting

**March 26, 2001:**
- **Final WCW Nitro airs** - "Night of Champions" from Panama City Beach, Florida
- Vince McMahon addressed WCW fans via pretaped segment
- Shane McMahon revealed he (not Vince) was the actual owner
- Booker T won the World Title from Scott Steiner
- Sting vs. Ric Flair was the final match in WCW history
- Tony Schiavone and Scott Hudson called the final broadcast
- JR hyped WWF's WrestleMania X-7 after the show

### Key Financial Figures:
- WCW lost approximately $62-80 million since summer 2000
- Fusient Media Ventures offered approximately $48.3-75 million
- WWF purchased WCW for approximately $7 million total ($3 million + $1.7 million tape library + lawsuit settlements)
- Fusient's offer was nearly 20 times higher than what WWF paid
- WCW had lost the Monday Night Wars ratings battle for 76+ consecutive weeks by late 1999

### What Could Have Been:
- Eric Bischoff had also received offers from Fox and NBC
- Fox made an offer to air WCW Nitro (just not on Monday nights)
- Fusient wanted control over time slots on TNT/TBS regardless of programming
- WCW received a $5 million deposit from Fusient that would have doubled what WWF paid

**Source:** https://wcwworldwide.com/2018/03/final-wcw-monday-nitro/

### Sting and Ric Flair's Final Match

**Incident:** The last match in WCW history was Sting vs. Ric Flair

**Details:**
- Both men were initially against the match - they were banged up and didn't want to play a role in such a tragic broadcast
- Vince McMahon personally requested that they get the last match
- Sting defeated Flair with the Scorpion Deathlock after a superplex (7:17)
- After the match, Sting and Flair embraced in the ring - a genuine moment of respect
- This closed the book on their legendary rivalry and on WCW itself

## Conflicts with Previous Reports

### October-December 2000 Report Updates
- **Scott Steiner's World Title reign:** The Oct-Dec report noted Steiner won the title on November 26, 2000. This is confirmed - he held it through January-March 2001 (120 days total), the longest reign since Goldberg's 1998 run.
- **Russo's concussion:** The Oct-Dec report noted Russo was concussed by Goldberg's spear (September 25). Russo returned to TV in January 2001 but was not a major presence in WCW's final months.
- **Bret Hart:** The Oct-Dec report noted Hart did not appear during October-December 2000. Hart continued to be absent in January-March 2001 (he had officially retired).
- **Booker T:** The Oct-Dec report noted Booker T won the title at Sin (January 14, 2001). This is consistent with the Jul-Sep and Oct-Dec reports' timeline.
- **Fusient Media Ventures:** The Oct-Dec report mentioned WCW searching for new ownership. The Fusient deal was announced January 11, 2001, but collapsed on March 19.
- **WWF purchase:** The Oct-Dec report mentioned WWF purchasing WCW for $4.2 million. More detailed figures suggest approximately $7 million total ($3 million for intangible properties plus tape library and lawsuit settlements).

## Conclusion

The January-March 2001 period was the death of World Championship Wrestling. Scott Steiner's Magnificent Seven dominated the final PPVs while the business collapsed behind the scenes. Three final PPVs were held (Sin, SuperBrawl Revenge, Greed), each marking another milestone in WCW's terminal decline. Eric Bischoff's Fusient Media Ventures attempted to save WCW with a $48-75 million offer, but Jamie Kellner's decision to cancel all WCW programming on TBS/TNT on March 19 destroyed the deal. Vince McMahon's WWF purchased WCW for approximately $7 million on March 23. The final Thunder aired March 21, and the final Nitro aired March 26 - a "Night of Champions" where Booker T won the World Title, becoming the final WCW World Champion. Sting and Ric Flair wrestled the last match in WCW history, embracing in the ring afterward. WCW, which had once dominated professional wrestling and won the Monday Night Wars, was no more. The company that Ted Turner built had been reduced to $7 million in assets purchased by its greatest rival.
