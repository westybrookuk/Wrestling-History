# WWF April-June 2003 Research Report

**Period Covered:** April 1, 2003 - June 30, 2003

## Executive Summary

The April-June 2003 period was a pivotal quarter for WWE, featuring three major PPV events — Backlash (April 27, Worcester), Judgment Day (May 18, Charlotte), and Bad Blood (June 15, Houston) — that defined the Ruthless Aggression era. Key developments included: Goldberg making his WWE in-ring debut and defeating The Rock at Backlash; the WWE Intercontinental Championship being reactivated by Steve Austin on May 5, 2003; Christian winning a battle royal to become the first IC Champion of the reactivated era at Judgment Day; Hulk Hogan appearing as "Mr. America" in disguise and passing a lie detector test; Randy Orton returning from injury as a masked man on May 26; La Résistance winning the World Tag Team Championship at Bad Blood; Triple H defeating Kevin Nash in a Hell in a Cell match at Bad Blood with Mick Foley as special guest referee; and Bad Blood becoming the first brand-exclusive PPV (Raw only). Backlash drew 10,000 attendance with 345,000 PPV buys and $450,000 gross. Judgment Day drew 13,000 attendance. Bad Blood drew 10,000 attendance with 385,000 buys and $500,000+ gross.

## Injuries / Backstage Incidents / Controversies

### Intercontinental Championship Reactivation
On May 5, 2003, Raw Co-General Manager Steve Austin reactivated the WWE Intercontinental Championship, which had been deactivated/unified at No Mercy 2002 (October 20, 2002). Austin declared any former Intercontinental Champion on the Raw roster eligible to enter a battle royal at Judgment Day. Christian won the battle royal to become the first champion of the reactivated era. This was done to provide a secondary singles title for the Raw brand, complementing the World Heavyweight Championship.

### Goldberg's Dominant Debut
Goldberg made his WWE in-ring debut at Backlash on April 27, 2003, defeating The Rock in a highly anticipated match. His debut was one of the most heavily pushed in WWE history, with an immediate main event push and feud with Chris Jericho following. Goldberg's "Who's Next?" catchphrase and dominant squash-match style were heavily promoted.

### Mr. America Identity Controversy
Hulk Hogan returned to WWE as "Mr. America" in disguise, exploiting a loophole in his suspension from Vince McMahon. The character was an open secret — everyone knew it was Hogan, but the storyline pretended otherwise. Vince McMahon was obsessed with exposing Mr. America as Hogan. On June 19, 2003, Mr. America passed a lie detector test when asked "Are you Hulk Hogan?" — answering "No." Vince refused to believe the results. On June 26, Vince showed footage proving Mr. America was Hogan and "fired" him (Mr. America quit instead).

### Zach Gowen Storyline
Zach Gowen, a one-legged wrestler, debuted on May 15, 2003 as a planted fan who tried to help Mr. America. Roddy Piper pulled off Gowen's prosthetic leg during the segment. Gowen allied with Mr. America and feuded with Piper and Sean O'Haire. On June 26, Vince McMahon told Gowen to join his "Kiss My Ass Club" (kiss McMahon's buttocks) in exchange for a WWE contract. Gowen attacked McMahon instead, turning face.

### Brand-Exclusive PPVs Begin
Bad Blood (June 15, 2003) was the first brand-exclusive PPV (Raw only), marking the beginning of non-Big Four PPVs being brand-exclusive. This continued until 2007 when brand-exclusive PPVs ended.

## Conclusion

The April-June 2003 period was defined by Goldberg's dominant WWE debut, the reactivation of the Intercontinental Championship, and the ongoing Evolution dominance on Raw. Backlash featured Goldberg's first WWE match, a victory over The Rock that set up one of the most heavily pushed debuts in WWE history. The Rock then left for Hollywood, clearing the way for Goldberg's ascent.

Judgment Day was notable for the reactivated Intercontinental Championship battle royal (won by Christian), Eddie Guerrero and Tajiri's ladder match victory for the WWE Tag Team Championship, and the Mr. America/Zach Gowen storyline reaching its peak. The Mr. America character — Hogan in disguise — passed a lie detector test on June 19 before Vince McMahon eventually "exposed" him on June 26.

Bad Blood marked a significant structural change as the first brand-exclusive PPV (Raw only). Triple H retained the World Heavyweight Championship in a Hell in a Cell match vs. Kevin Nash with Mick Foley as special guest referee. Goldberg continued his dominant push by defeating Chris Jericho. La Résistance won the World Tag Team Championship, establishing themselves as a major heel tag team.

By the end of June 2003, the championship landscape was: Brock Lesnar (WWE Champion, SmackDown!), Triple H (World Heavyweight Champion, Raw), Christian (Intercontinental Champion, Raw), La Résistance (World Tag Team Champions, Raw), Eddie Guerrero & Tajiri (WWE Tag Team Champions, SmackDown!), Jazz (Women's Champion, Raw), and Matt Hardy (Cruiserweight Champion, SmackDown!). Goldberg was the top contender on Raw, and Evolution remained the dominant faction.
